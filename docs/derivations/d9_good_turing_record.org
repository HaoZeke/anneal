* D9: Good-Turing × record discovery value
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: d9-good-turing-record
:END:

** Scope and quantity
Restart endpoints are registered into basins (merge radius \(5\%\) of the
normalized box). Let \(n\) be the number of registered endpoints, \(w\) the
number of distinct basins, and \(n_1\) the number of *singleton* basins
(seen once). The quantity optimized is the per-slice probability that one
more restart both discovers an *unseen* basin and that this basin beats the
incumbent — the distribution-free exploration term of the portfolio win
objective \(W\) (paper §Allocation and endgame).

** Assumptions
1. Restart endpoints are i.i.d. draws from a fixed basin-of-attraction
   measure on the box (restart-arm model).
2. Basin depths (best objective values) of distinct basins are exchangeable.
3. The merge rule identifies basins correctly up to the fixed radius
   (implementation detail; the identities below treat basin labels as given).

** Good-Turing missing mass
*** Proposition D9.1 (Good 1953)
The frequency-of-frequencies estimator
\[
\hat{M}=\frac{n_1}{n}
\tag{D9.1}
\]
is unbiased for the total probability mass of *unseen* types under sampling
with replacement from a discrete measure (here: basins). McAllester–Schapire
concentration bounds control \(\lvert\hat{M}-M\rvert\) for large \(n\); the
portfolio uses \(\hat{M}\) as a point estimator once \(n\ge 5\).

** Record probability under exchangeability
*** Proposition D9.2
If basin depths \(D_1,\ldots,D_w,D_{\mathrm{new}}\) are exchangeable and a.s.
have a unique minimum, then
\[
\mathbb{P}\bigl(D_{\mathrm{new}}<\min\{D_1,\ldots,D_w\}\bigr)
=\frac{1}{w+1}.
\tag{D9.2}
\]

/Proof./ Among \(w+1\) exchangeable values the index of the unique minimum is
uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,w+1\}\). \(\square\)

** Discovery value
*** Definition D9.3
The per-slice *discovery value* is
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{disc}}
  =\hat{M}\cdot\frac{1}{w+1}
  =\frac{n_1}{n\,(w+1)}.
\tag{D9.3}
\end{equation}
Interpretation: probability that one more restart finds a new basin *and*
that basin improves the incumbent record, under (A1)–(A2).

*** Corollary D9.4 (thin-registry abstention)
For \(n<5\) the estimator is withheld and the portfolio falls back to
aggregated Beta posteriors over improvement bits (D4). This is an
engineering gate, not part of the asymptotic estimator.

** Algorithm D9 (record-discovery gate)
Input: basin registry with hits \(h_j\) for basin \(j=1,\ldots,w\), total
samples \(n=\sum_j h_j\).
1. If \(n<5\), return \(\bot\) (use Beta fallback).
2. Set \(n_1=\#\{j:h_j=1\}\), \(w=\#\) basins.
3. Return \(\theta_{\mathrm{disc}}=n_1/(n(w+1))\).

** Composition with endgame
In the win objective of D10, \(q_0=1-\theta_{\mathrm{disc}}\) is the probability
the incumbent *survives* one more exploration challenge; the probability that
\(e\) further slices still fail to improve is
\((1-\theta_{\mathrm{disc}})^{e}\) under independence of slices.
