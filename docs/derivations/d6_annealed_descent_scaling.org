* D6: Annealed Metropolis state-drift scaling
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: d6-annealed-metropolis-state-drift-scaling
:END:
** Scope and quantity being optimized
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scope-and-quantity-being-optimized
:END:
Let (f(x)=|x|^2/2) and propose
(y=x+sigma z), where (zsim N(0,I_d)). Metropolis accepts a
change (Delta=f(y)-f(x)) with probability

\[
a_T(\Delta)=\min\{1,\exp(-\Delta/T)\}.
\]

This derivation concerns the expected decrease of the *current chain
state*,

\[
G=-\mathbb{E}[\Delta a_T(\Delta)].
\tag{D6.1}
\]

It does not describe the one-step improvement of the best-so-far
incumbent. An incumbent accepts every improving proposal into its record
and ignores every worsening proposal, so its one-step expected improvement
is (-\mathbb{E}[\Delta;\Delta<0]), independent of (T). Temperature
matters to optimization through the location of the evolving chain and
its ability to leave a basin, not through the record update itself.

** High-dimensional sphere limit
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: high-dimensional-sphere-limit
:END:
Use the evolution-strategy scaling
(sigma=c|x|/d). Since

\[
\Delta=\sigma x^\top z+\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\|z\|^2,
\]

normalizing energy changes by (f(x)/d), applying
(|z|^2/d\to 1), and using the Gaussian law of
(x^\top z/|x|) gives the limiting increment

\[
D:=\frac{d\Delta}{f(x)}\ \Longrightarrow\
N(c^2,4c^2).
\tag{D6.2}
\]

Define the dimensionless temperature
(	heta=Td/f(x)). The normalized state gain is

\[
G(c,\theta)=-\mathbb{E}[D\min\{1,e^{-D/\theta}\}].
\tag{D6.3}
\]

Equation (D6.2) is an asymptotic model, not a finite-dimensional identity.
Claims below are exact within that limiting model.

** Closed form
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: closed-form
:END:
Let (mu=c^2), (s=2c), and
(mu'=\mu-s^2/\theta). Standard truncated-normal identities give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[D;D\le0]
  &=\mu\Phi(-\mu/s)-s\phi(\mu/s),\\
\mathbb{E}[De^{-D/\theta};D>0]
  &=e^{s^2/(2\theta^2)-\mu/\theta}
    \{\mu'\Phi(\mu'/s)+s\phi(\mu'/s)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{D6.4}
\]

Substitution in (D6.3) yields the objective used by
=proofs/d6_annealed_descent_scaling.py=. The same exponential tilt gives
the acceptance probability

\[
A(c,\theta)=\Phi(-\mu/s)+
e^{s^2/(2\theta^2)-\mu/\theta}\Phi(\mu'/s).
\tag{D6.5}
\]

** Exact critical temperature in the limiting model
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: exact-critical-temperature-in-the-limiting-model
:END:
*** Proposition D6.1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: proposition-d6.1
:END:
For every (c>0), the gain in (D6.3) is positive when
(0<\theta<2), zero when (	heta=2), and negative when
(	heta>2).

*Proof.* Let (p_c) be the density of (N(c^2,4c^2)). For (u>0),

\[
\log\frac{p_c(-u)}{p_c(u)}
=-\frac{(-u-c^2)^2-(u-c^2)^2}{8c^2}
=-\frac{u}{2}.
\tag{D6.6}
\]

Pairing the (+u) and (-u) contributions in (D6.3) therefore gives

\[
G(c,\theta)=\int_0^\infty u p_c(u)
\{e^{-u/2}-e^{-u/\theta}\}\,du.
\tag{D6.7}
\]

The weight (u p_c(u)) is strictly positive. If (	heta<2), then
(e^{-u/2}>e^{-u/\theta}) for every (u>0); equality holds at
(	heta=2); and the inequality reverses for (	heta>2).
(qquad\blacksquare)

The proof is global in (c). The series

\[
G(c,\theta)=c^2\frac{2-\theta}{\theta}+O(c^3)
\tag{D6.8}
\]

is retained as an independent symbolic check, but is not used to infer
the global sign.

** Anisotropic local quadratic
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: anisotropic-local-quadratic
:END:
For (f(x)=x^\top Hx/2), (H\succ0), and an isotropic small proposal
(y=x+\sigma z), the leading Gaussian increment has

\[
\mu=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}H,
\qquad
s^2=\sigma^2x^\top H^2x.
\]

The small-step expansion changes sign at

\[
T_c(x)=\frac{x^\top H^2x}{\operatorname{tr}H}.
\tag{D6.9}
\]

Unlike Proposition D6.1, (D6.9) is a local small-step result. A proposal
with covariance proportional to (H^{-1}) whitens the quadratic and
restores (T_c=2f(x)/d). This is the mathematical reason to adapt
proposal shape separately from proposal size.

** Executable witnesses and software implication
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: executable-witnesses-and-software-implication
:END:
The SymPy witness verifies the truncated-normal identities, the series
coefficient, the density ratio and paired-integrand factorizations, and
the anisotropic reduction. Numerical quadrature and one-dimensional
optimization evaluate the constants. At (	heta=0.5), the limiting
sphere model gives (c^*\simeq1.326), (G^*\simeq0.368), and
(A^*\simeq0.320).

The portfolio's adaptive-Metropolis arm uses (	heta=0.5) as a
model-derived local operating point and tracks the associated acceptance
rate. This is a conditional design rule for locally quadratic basins. It
is not a universal cooling theorem and does not replace the logarithmic
global-convergence conditions for classical simulated annealing.
