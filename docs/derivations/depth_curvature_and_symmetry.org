#+TITLE: What curvature and symmetry buy the quench-depth predictor, and what they do not
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:t

* Abstract

The delayed-acceptance first stage (=src/delayed.rs=) regresses the depth a
quench recovers, \(1/2\, g^\top H^{-1} g\) to leading order, and the model
Hessian in =src/model_hessian.rs= stands in for \(H\) because the true
Hessian is not on the ledger. Three questions follow: does the exact
Hessian predict depth better than the model does; does carrying curvature
across a hop by symmetry pay for itself; and does calibrating the model
online, from the descents a search already generates, close any of the
gap. Measured on branches =feat/sym-hessian= (commit =be4c868=) and
=feat/hessian-fit= (commit =9ae6076=), against the corrected relaxer. All
three answers are no, yes-but-not-worth-it, and only at short steps,
respectively, and each rests on a measurement rather than a premise taken
on faith.

* 1. The anharmonicity ceiling

=src/sym_hessian.rs= (=be4c868=) scores four depth predictors against 240
targets sampled around a relaxed LJ38 minimum, after the one global force
constant scale the downstream regression absorbs in every case:

| predictor | median relative depth error | Spearman rank correlation |
|-----------+------------------------------+----------------------------|
| exact Hessian at the target | 0.103 | 0.991 |
| free model Hessian | 0.159 | 0.999 |
| \(1/2\lvert g\rvert^2\) | 0.189 | 0.999 |
| exact Hessian, transported | 0.557 | -- |

The exact Hessian improves median error by 0.06 over the free model and *loses*
rank correlation doing it, 0.991 against 0.999. A predictor built from the
true second derivative should not lose to a stretch-only model with a
diagonal floor standing in for the terms it omits, and the reason is where
the two operators differ, not by how much.

Off the three translations, which is all the depth solve deflates, the
softest four eigenvalues of the exact Hessian at the LJ38 reference are
\(-4.3\times10^{-8},\ 3.1\times10^{-8},\ 1.1\times10^{-7},\ 3.3\): the three
rigid rotations survive undamped, so the operator the solve inverts is
numerically singular in that subspace. The model's corresponding four are
\(0.05,\ 0.05,\ 0.05,\ 1.1\), the first three being =FLOOR * K0= exactly,
the diagonal shift that stands in for the bending terms a stretch-only
force constant omits. The shift the model was given for a different reason,
because it has no angular response, is what keeps its inverse away from a
near-singular direction the exact operator walks straight into.

The reading is that depth prediction is limited by anharmonicity and not by
the curvature model: a Hessian with zero transport error still misses by
0.103 relative, and no operator built from a single evaluation point closes
that, because the quench travels through terrain the local quadratic form
does not see. A better second-derivative operator has nothing to win here.

* 2. Two negatives, pinned as tests

Same commit, two uses of symmetry for the depth solve, both built, both
measured, and both correct without paying.

** Block-diagonalisation

Site symmetries of a structure give an orthogonal representation the model
Hessian commutes with, so the operator splits by irreducible representation
and the class sums reach the isotypic decomposition without building a
character table. Depths recombine to the plain depth to \(7\times10^{-14}\).
Serial cost is unchanged on an icosahedron and a centred cuboctahedron and
1.33 times worse on a truncated octahedron; the critical path falls 4.5 to
5.0 times, which needs a core per block on a search that already spends its
cores on thousands of independent hops rather than on parallelising one.
Worst-block condition number is 93 against 150 for the whole operator. A
\(C_1\) control gets one block and the same iteration count, bitwise.

** Curvature transport by correspondence

A permutation and rotation carrying a target onto a reference transports the
curvature as \(M^\top H_A M\), exactly: \(3\times10^{-16}\) onto a
relabelled copy. Over 240 targets around the same LJ38 minimum, operator
transfer error runs 0.016 at an IRA match distance of 0.003 and saturates
near 0.9 past a distance of 0.15, beating rebuilding the model at every
distance measured (0.98).

That curve is also what prices the correspondence out. Below an IRA
distance of 0.17 the transported and the identity correspondences agree to
three decimal places, because a small perturbation leaves IRA returning the
identity permutation and a near-identity rotation: the correspondence
contributes nothing exactly where transport works. Past a distance of 1.6,
where it does contribute, it moves the operator error from 0.820 to 0.725
on an operator that is already three quarters wrong. At 0.41 ms a call,
against a measured 52 charged evaluations per hop for IRA where the cheap
descriptor costs 31, that is a cost with nothing behind it at either end.

Both results are pinned as tests on =feat/sym-hessian= so they fail loudly
if either stops being true.

* 3. Online calibration helps only where the harmonic form applies

=examples/hessian_fit_lj.rs= (=feat/hessian-fit=, commit =9ae6076=) fits
the model's force-constant decay and bending floor on the first 120
descents of a perturbed relaxed cluster and scores median relative depth
error on the next 120, both arms carrying their own fitted scale so the
comparison is shape against shape rather than shape against a scale
mismatch:

| step | LJ38 default | LJ38 fitted | improvement | LJ75 default | LJ75 fitted | improvement |
|------+---------------+-------------+-------------+---------------+-------------+-------------|
| 0.08 |         0.175 |       0.075 | 2.33 times  |         0.145 |       0.048 | 3.00 times  |
| 0.15 |         0.174 |       0.148 | 1.18 times  |         0.114 |       0.091 | 1.26 times  |
| 0.25 |         0.090 |       0.072 | 1.25 times  |         0.058 |       0.048 | 1.19 times  |
| 0.35 |         0.081 |       0.066 | 1.23 times  |         0.047 |       0.038 | 1.22 times  |

The improvement concentrates where the harmonic form has something to fit:
at step 0.08 the fit returns a force-constant decay near 15 on LJ38 and near
20 on LJ75 against a default of 1, close to what a Lennard-Jones pair force
constant looks like written as an exponential, falling off far faster than
a covalent bond's and passing through zero at the well's inflection point.

The search does not run at step 0.08; it runs at 0.25 to 0.35, where the
improvement is about 1.2 times. At that step the descent recovers
anharmonic energy no quadratic form accounts for, the true objective over
the whole search box runs 0.074 to 0.089 regardless of the fitted
constants, and calibration correctly reports that no shape helps much once
the surface is that flat.

An online accumulator, fed the same training half one descent at a time and
holding only its last 48, comes within a few per cent of the batch fit
everywhere and beats it on LJ75 at step 0.08, 0.0454 against 0.0482. Where
the objective is flat it settles on different constants for the same
held-out error, LJ38 at 0.25 giving decay 5.6 and floor 2.3 online against
1.8 and 1.2 in batch, both scoring 0.072: the fit is not unique where the
descent no longer constrains it. Every descent scored is one the search
already generates for its own reasons, so the calibration charges nothing
on the ledger.

* 4. What this means for the delayed-acceptance surrogate

Section 1 bounds what any curvature-based depth feature can improve in the
first stage in =src/delayed.rs=, independent of the design-matrix fault the
scaled raw-energy column produces (the eleventh case in the campaign notes
kept in the paper repository, =simmAnnealTeX/rewrite_2026/docs/inert_mechanisms.org=):
even a design that could carry information would still face a 0.103
relative-error floor from anharmonicity, before any question of how the
surrogate is fitted. Sections 2 and 3 are why the exact Hessian and
structural symmetry stay out of the depth solve, correct and priced and
not worth carrying.
