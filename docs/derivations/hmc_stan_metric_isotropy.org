#+TITLE: Why Stan's mass-matrix adaptation fails on a cluster target, structurally
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:t

* Status

Branch =feat/hmc-hop=, six commits =3584888..eadd09c=, not merged and not
pushed. =src/hmc/{metric,dual_average,hop}.rs= carry the full derivation and
measurement in their module documentation; this note draws the argument out
of three files into one place and adds nothing the source does not already
state. Placement question addressed in Section 6.

* Abstract

Hamiltonian Monte Carlo replaces basin hopping's uniform kick with a
trajectory: direction from the gradient, step size from dual averaging
against a target acceptance, trajectory length from the no-U-turn
criterion. What is left for a human to choose is the mass matrix, and this
branch measures three choices on the hopping chain rather than picking
one by hand. The result is not that HMC loses to the crate's own move
library, though it does. It is *why* Stan's own adaptation, sample-covariance
estimation of
the metric, fails on this class of target, and the reason is structural
rather than a matter of more data: a cluster potential is invariant under
permuting indistinguishable atoms and under global rotation, so its
population covariance is isotropic by symmetry, and a sample estimate of an
isotropic quantity is estimating nothing but its own noise.

* 1. The structural argument

A cluster potential's value depends on the point set only through pairwise
distances. Two consequences follow exactly, not approximately: relabelling
identical atoms leaves the potential unchanged, and rotating every
coordinate about the centre of mass leaves it unchanged. Both are exact
symmetries of the target density, not properties of one structure, so the
population covariance of the target inherits them and is isotropic, a
scalar times the identity.

Stan's Euclidean metric (Stan reference manual, MCMC chapter, "Euclidean
metric") estimates the inverse mass matrix from the regularised sample
covariance of the draws taken so far. That estimator is built for targets
whose coordinates carry different scales from each other, and it is the
wrong tool here: whatever anisotropy it reports on a cluster target is a
finite-sample artefact of which structures the chain happened to visit,
never a property of the target it estimates. More samples do not fix this,
because the quantity being estimated does not have the shape the estimator
requires.

The crate has an alternative the general-purpose sampler does not: a model
Hessian (=src/model_hessian.rs=) read off the current geometry at zero cost
on the evaluation ledger, giving a mass matrix with the target's own
curvature instead of an estimate of noise.

* 2. What was measured

LJ38 at \(4\times10^5\) charged evaluations, 24 seeds an arm, Wilson 95 per
cent intervals, corrected relaxer (=src/hmc/hop.rs=, commit =844c448=):

| arm | proposal | solved | 95% CI | charged/hop |
|-----------+----------------------------------+--------+-------------+------|
| uniform   | the crate's five-kernel library  | 14/24 | [0.39, 0.76] | 41 |
| hmc-hess  | trajectory, model Hessian metric | 6/24  | [0.12, 0.45] | 76 |
| hmc       | trajectory, \(M = I\)            | 5/24  | [0.09, 0.41] | 84 |
| kick      | the uniform displacement alone   | 1/24  | [0.01, 0.20] | 29 |
| hmc-diag  | trajectory, Stan's sample diagonal | 0/24 | [0.00, 0.14] | 148 |

Two comparisons, and only one of them settles anything. Against the uniform
kick it replaces, the Hamiltonian proposal is ahead on the point estimate,
6/24 and 5/24 against 1/24, but neither gap clears a two-sided Fisher exact
test at 24 seeds (\(p=0.097\) and \(p=0.19\)), and the posterior comparison
(paper repository's =rewrite_2026/scripts/posterior.py=, Jeffreys prior) is
the sharper instrument for why: the model-Hessian arm reaches
\(P(t>c)=0.982\), which reads as strong taken alone, against a Bayes factor
of 2.16, barely worth mentioning. The two numbers disagree on purpose; the
Bayes factor is what says 24 seeds cannot yet tell this arm from the kick,
which the exceedance probability alone would hide. Against the library the
driver runs by default, which adds surface relocation, shell rotation, and
symmetrisation to the kick, the trajectory loses and the loss is
significant (\(p=0.039\) and \(p=0.017\); posterior BF 3.74 for the
model-Hessian arm, moderate evidence for a difference in the losing
direction). Replacing the whole move library with Hamiltonian dynamics
gives up more than the dynamics returns, and matched against only the
kernel it was built to replace it is not yet a win, only not yet a loss.

That divide is consistent with the pattern documented across this crate's
other measured mechanisms (=docs/inert_mechanisms.org= in the paper
repository catalogues sixteen; this is not one of the first thirteen,
nothing here fails to fire): what pays is changing what a hop can reach, and
a trajectory matched to the kick's reach reaches exactly as far as the
kick, in a better-informed direction, while surface relocation and
symmetrisation place a point somewhere a displacement of that amplitude
cannot reach at
all.

* 3. Dual averaging works; the metric matters by the amount theory predicts

Both the identity and model-Hessian arms froze at an acceptance statistic
of 0.801 against a target of 0.800, from step sizes found without a human
sweep, with seed-to-seed spread of 2.1e-2 to 2.6e-2 (identity) and 4.9e-2 to
5.8e-2 (model Hessian). Dual averaging (Hoffman and Gelman, "The No-U-Turn
Sampler", JMLR 15 (2014) 1593-1623, algorithms 5 and 6) does what it is
built to do.

The model Hessian carries a condition number of 1.3e2 over the structures
visited and reaches a step 2.4 times larger at the same acceptance target,
a preconditioner's job. Stan's estimated diagonal carries a
condition number reaching 1.2e3, and 4.4e3 at the extreme: not curvature,
by the argument in Section 1, but finite-sample noise around an isotropic
target. Under that mass matrix dual averaging cannot reach its target
either, freezing at an acceptance of 0.934 against 0.800, the trajectory
under-reaches at 1.35 against a target of 2.34, and the arm never leaves
the icosahedral funnel across 24 seeds: deepest structure -173.134317, the
second minimum, where every other arm reaches -173.928427 at least once.
Stan cannot see this coming, because Stan carries no model of its target
and so has no way to know the covariance it is estimating is a constant
times the identity.

* 4. The no-U-turn criterion does not fire

The depth cap (=HopConfig::max_depth=, default 5, 31 leaves) bound on 99.2
per cent of identity proposals and 95.5 per cent of model-Hessian ones.
Raising it does not help: swept from 4 to 10, the cap still bound on 87 to
99 per cent of proposals at every setting, at a cost rising to 1098 charged
evaluations per hop. The trajectory length is set by the cap, not by the
geometry the no-U-turn criterion is supposed to read, so the claim to have
removed the trajectory-length parameter does not hold: the reach adaptation
(Section 5 below) drives the trajectory time down as the cap rises, from
14.9 at a cap of 4 to 0.57 at a cap of 10, holding the distance covered
fixed, and a trajectory told to cover a fixed short distance in a
114-dimensional space does not turn around on its own. The two adaptive
rules are in conflict, and reach wins.

The preconditioner does move the number in the right direction: the
model-Hessian metric U-turns 5.7 times more often than the identity, which
is the sign theory predicts, since the U-turn time is set by the slowest
mode and the step size by the fastest, and a metric that closes the gap
between them makes the criterion reachable more often.

* 5. Momentum is matched on reach, not on energy

Neither of the two obvious ways to set the trajectory's energy scale
\(T_{\mathrm{traj}}\) works. The chain's own Metropolis temperature is
wrong by two orders of magnitude in the direction that destroys the
structure: 0.8, a threshold meant for differences between minima, injects
45.6 units of kinetic energy into a 38-point cluster against a pair well
depth of 1, which evaporates it rather than perturbing it. Matching the
energy the control's kick injects is also wrong: measured on a relaxed
13-point cluster at the library's half-width of 0.38, a uniform kick raises
the energy by a median of 3.3e3 and a mean of 1.4e5, driven by pairs pushed
onto the \(r^{-12}\) wall, which a quench absorbs and a trajectory cannot.

What the two proposals have in common, and what the whole campaign is
about, is how far they move the structure. \(T_{\mathrm{traj}}\) is
therefore adapted so the trajectory's rigid-mode-free displacement matches
the kick's, \(h\sqrt{\mathrm{dim}/3}\) for a uniform half-width \(h\),
by a damped Newton step in \(\log T_{\mathrm{traj}}\) run during warmup
alongside dual averaging. It charges nothing, is specific to no potential
or size, and is what lets Section 2's comparison be about direction rather
than about which arm happened to get a larger step.

* 6. Divergences are exactly where the potential says they should be

Rare, 4.0e-4 of proposals under both working metrics, and structural: they
occur at a closest-pair separation of 0.67 to 0.74 of the mean spacing,
which is the \(r^{-12}\) wall. A trajectory cannot cross a configuration
with a pair inside about three quarters of a contact distance; that is a
statement about the potential, transfers to any potential with a hard
core, and is not a statement about the integrator.

* 7. Placement

Not manuscript material yet. The branch sits unmerged and unpushed, and a
manuscript that claims a deposited reproduction pipeline regenerates its
tables should not cite a kernel that is not in the tagged source. The
module documentation for =hop.rs= also states plainly that the no-U-turn
criterion does not fire under the current reach-adaptation rule, Section 4
above, so calling this arm "NUTS" in a manuscript would report an
implementation as adaptive when it runs fixed-length HMC under that name;
that conflict wants resolving before the method is named in public.

The finding, once the branch merges and the cap/reach conflict is settled
one way or the other, is squarely on the paper's thesis: a domain-specific
mass matrix built at zero ledger cost beats a general-purpose sampler's own
adaptation on a target class where that adaptation is structurally blind,
and it does so for a stated, checked reason rather than an empirical one.
That argument does not depend on the implementation's remaining defects;
the comparison against the crate's own library and the plain accounting of
where the trajectory-length claim does not hold both do.
