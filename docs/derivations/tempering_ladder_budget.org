#+TITLE: The classical replica-exchange target is wrong under a fixed budget
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:t

* Status

Branch =feat/tempering-nrpt=, commits =35e3da9= through =368c69e=, not
merged and not pushed. =src/tempering.rs= and the =LadderMode= doc in
=src/methods/cluster_hopping.rs= carry the full derivation and measurement;
this note draws them into one place. Placement addressed in Section 5.

* Abstract

Classical parallel tempering places rungs by fixing the temperature range
an application needs and equalising the communication barrier between
neighbours, free to add as many rungs as that equalisation calls for. A
fixed-budget optimiser cannot add rungs without paying for them: every rung is a chain
paid for out of the same charged-evaluation budget as every other. Under
that constraint the derivation that motivates the classical target still
holds exactly, and the conclusion it is normally read to license, that a
longer ladder is better, reverses: adding rungs divides a fixed number of
sweeps among more chains faster than the added rungs shorten the walk each
tagged configuration has to make, so round trips per run fall rather than
rise. The ladder that pursues the classical target measurably costs solve
rate at fixed budget rather than buying it, and the corrected non-adaptive
alternative gains nothing either. Neither result is a bug in the ladder; both
are what the classical target's own derivation predicts once its free
variable is exchanged for a fixed one.

* 1. What the classical target takes as given, and what a fixed budget fixes instead

The communication-barrier construction (=Ladder::equalise=,
=beta_step_for_acceptance=, =TARGET_SWAP_ACCEPT = 0.23=) places rungs so
neighbouring pairs share one rejection rate, the acceptance target the
literature calls optimal for round-trip time per sweep. That derivation
takes the temperature range as given and the rung count as the thing being
solved for: however many rungs equalising the barrier calls for, the
ladder gets. A campaign under a charged-evaluation ledger has the opposite
free variable. The budget is fixed, the rung count is a choice charged
against it, and every rung added is a chain the budget now divides its
hops among instead of a chain added on top.

The same identity that gives the round-trip time its favourable scaling in
rung count, measured on the idealised index process at a fixed barrier of
3 (=src/tempering.rs=, =IndexProcess=, sweeps per tagged round trip):

| rungs | deterministic sweep | stochastic sweep | one drawn pair |
|-------+----------------------+-------------------+-----------------|
|     8 |                   99 |               197 |             675 |
|    16 |                  153 |               568 |            4241 |
|    32 |                  281 |              2175 |           36863 |
|    64 |                  560 |              8619 |          273792 |

which fits \(N^{0.84}\), \(N^{1.83}\) and \(N^{2.91}\), reproducing the
published separation between a deterministic even-odd sweep (Syed,
Bouchard-Cote, Deligiannidis and Doucet) and the classical drawn-pair
scheme at roughly \(O(N)\) against \(O(N^2)\). Nothing about that scaling
is wrong. It answers "how many sweeps does one round trip need," which is
the right question when sweeps are free. They are not free here.

* 2. Inside the driver, where sweeps are not free

The same measurement repeated where the rungs share one fixed budget
instead of each having its own, LJ38 at \(4\times10^5\) charged
evaluations, a swap every ten hops, 24 seeds, round trips completed per
run:

| rungs | sweeps the budget affords | stochastic | deterministic |
|-------+--------------------------+-------------+------------------|
|     4 |                      270 |        29.0 |             34.0 |
|     8 |                      139 |         7.1 |             17.5 |
|    16 |                       69 |         0.4 |              4.7 |
|    32 |                       35 |         0.0 |              0.0 |

The deterministic sweep still leads, by a factor of 2.5 at eight rungs and
12 at sixteen, exactly what the scaling in Section 1 predicts. The lead
does not survive contact with the budget. A run of fixed cost divides its
hops among the rungs, so the sweeps it can afford fall as \(1/N\) while the
per-tag round-trip time rises as \(N\) (Section 1's near-linear fit).
Round trips per run therefore fall as \(1/N^2\) for the deterministic
sweep and \(1/N^3\) for the reversible one, and by 32 rungs neither
completes a single round trip. A longer ladder is not available to a
fixed-budget optimiser whatever the sweep scheme does with the sweeps it
gets.

* 3. Measured cost: the adapted ladder costs solve rate, past merely wasting it

LJ38 at \(4\times10^5\) charged evaluations, 96 seeds, four rungs, a swap
every ten hops, against a single chain at the same budget (full table and
sourcing in the paper repository's =inert_mechanisms.org=, entries
fourteen and fifteen):

| mode | solved | 95% Wilson |
|------+--------+------------|
| Cyclic (exchange, own temperatures) | 77/96 | [0.71,0.87] |
| single chain | 70/96 | [0.63,0.81] |
| Independent (own temperatures, no exchange) | 67/96 | [0.60,0.78] |
| NonReversible (adapted to the classical 0.23 target) | 45/96 | [0.37,0.57] |

The adapted ladder, the one that pursues the classical communication-barrier
target, is the only arm whose interval separates from the single chain,
and it separates downward: Fisher's exact gives 0.0004, which survives
Bonferroni correction over the seventeen arms this campaign has compared.
Posterior comparison (paper repository's =rewrite_2026/scripts/posterior.py=,
Jeffreys prior) agrees at a different order of confidence: \(P(t>c)=0.0001\),
diff \(-0.258\) [-0.388,-0.123], Bayes factor 110.7, strong evidence for a
difference and the difference is the harmful direction. Morse at
\(\rho=6\), same budget, 48 seeds, agrees on the sign though not the
ranking among the other arms (=inert_mechanisms.org= again).

The mechanism is direct. Equalising the barrier at 0.23 stretches the hot
rung to a mean temperature of 61 against 3.2 for a plain geometric
schedule at the same rung count, and a rung that hot holds nothing worth
swapping down while still consuming a quarter of the fixed budget on
sweeps that cannot cross to a useful configuration. Raising the target to
0.40 and 0.60 shortens the ladder and recovers the solve rate to 0.64 and
0.54, still no better than the single chain it replaces: less harmful, not
helpful. The target the classical derivation optimises for, round-trip
time per sweep at an unconstrained rung count, is not the quantity a
fixed-budget optimiser needs minimised.

* 4. The ladder does not rescue a Hamiltonian rung either

A related question this branch also measured and closed: whether pairing
the ladder with the Hamiltonian trajectory proposal of =feat/hmc-hop=
(=anneal/docs/derivations/hmc_stan_metric_isotropy.org=) combines two
partial mechanisms into one that pays. It does not, for the same budget
reason. A Hamiltonian rung costs 76 charged evaluations per hop against 41
for the crate's own move library, so a run of \(4\times10^5\) takes 5216 hops
against 9781; at a swap every ten hops over four rungs that is 130 sweeps
against 245, already down where sixteen displacement rungs sit at 4.7
round trips per run (Section 2). The depth cap binds on 86.8 per cent of
proposals under the identity metric and 78.8 per cent under the model
Hessian even at a cap of ten, so a Hamiltonian rung on this ladder would
carry an effectively fixed-length trajectory with no U-turn-driven length
to trade against the reduced sweep count. Two mechanisms that are
individually absent from the outcome, Section 3 here and
=hmc_stan_metric_isotropy.org= Section 2 there, do not have a product
worth a budget line.

* 5. Placement

Not manuscript material yet: the branch is unmerged and unpushed, matching
the HMC note's reasoning in its own Section 7. The result is stated here in
full because, unlike the HMC finding, nothing about the implementation is
in question; the classical-target derivation and the code both do exactly
what they are supposed to, and the finding is that the quantity the
classical target optimises is the wrong one for this constraint. That
makes it, once landed, a companion result to the screening-pass finding
already in the manuscript's Table~tbl:reference: another instance of a
published tuning target built on a resource this campaign does not have
without paying for it.
