#+TITLE: The perturbation in the Hessian metric
#+OPTIONS: toc:2

* Why isotropic perturbations are mostly wasted

The measured fact this responds to: every funnel crossing observed arrives in a
single perturbation followed by a single quench, and no reweighting of which
draws are kept moves the crossing probability. The remaining freedom is the
draw itself.

Expand the energy at a minimum, \(E(x_0 + d) \approx E_0 + \tfrac12 d^T H d\),
with eigenpairs \((\lambda_i, v_i)\) of the projected Hessian. An isotropic draw
\(d\) spreads its squared norm evenly over all \(3n - 6\) directions. The quench
that follows relaxes every component back down its own parabola; a component
survives, in the sense of carrying the chain over a ridge, only where the rise
it bought is comparable to a barrier. Along a stiff mode the same displacement
buys \(\lambda_{\text{stiff}} / \lambda_{\text{soft}}\) times the energy, which
for a Lennard-Jones minimum spans two orders of magnitude, so stiff components
are spent on energy that the screen rejects or the quench reabsorbs.

The draw that spends the same energy budget in every direction it uses is
\(c_i \sim N(0, T / \lambda_i)\): equipartition in the quadratic model, the
local Gaussian \(N(0, T H^{-1})\). Truncated to the \(k\) softest non-rigid
modes it concentrates the whole step where displacement is cheap, and the
truncation error is the modes whose thermal amplitude was negligible anyway.

* The linear algebra

The subspace comes from the crate's shifted Lanczos: one pass to bound the
spectrum, a second on \(\sigma I - H\) so the softest end converges first, full
reorthogonalisation, rigid modes projected out of the operator and the Krylov
vectors both. Hessian action by central difference of the gradient, two charged
evaluations per product; the six translation and rotation modes never enter.
`curvature::soft_subspace` returns the \(k\) softest Ritz pairs, tested
against a dense projected reference.

Eigenvalues are floored at 0.05 in the amplitude so a shoulder, where
\(\lambda \to 0\), proposes a finite step rather than a blow-up.

* What separates this from the arms already refuted

The single softest mode as a displacement was measured in this crate and
failed: 6494 of 6550 trials died in the screen, because one parabola climbed at
fixed amplitude is all rise. The subspace draw is a different object: \(k\)
modes at thermal amplitudes, expected rise \(k T / 2\), comparable to an
ordinary accepted hop.

Against the oracle arms: nothing here mentions a morphology. The subspace is
the incumbent structure's own curvature, recomputed when the chain moves and
cached while it stands still, and the identical machinery runs on any system
with a gradient.

* Cost, honestly

A subspace at 30 Lanczos steps costs about 120 charged evaluations, four
ordinary hops. Measured in the smoke run the arm roughly halves the hop count
at fixed budget. The bet is the same one the screen calibration already
settled in the other direction: whether fewer, better-aimed draws beat more,
blind ones. That is what the campaign measures.

* Measured: harmful, and not only through its cost

72 seeds at 38 points, 48 at each gate, identical seeds and budgets to the
control:

| system | control | soft subspace |
|--------+---------+---------------|
| LJ38   |   55/72 |         32/72 |
| LJ75   |    4/48 |          1/48 |
| LJ98   |    5/48 |          1/48 |

The cost is real: 977 subspace computations per run at about 120 evaluations
each is 29% of the budget, and the hop count falls 39%. But the loss exceeds
the cost. By the crossing distribution measured earlier, diverting 29% of the
budget predicts roughly 42 to 47 of 72; the arm lands at 32. The aimed draws
are not crossing better than blind ones, they are crossing worse, while a
sixth of all proposals are spent on them.

The physical reading: the soft modes of a Lennard-Jones minimum are surface
rearrangements and collective distortions *within* the funnel. The interfunnel
rearrangement at 38 points is not a soft harmonic direction of the incumbent;
it is a large discrete rearrangement whose direction at the minimum is stiff.
Equipartition in the local quadratic model aims the step at exactly the
transitions that stay inside the funnel, which is worse than not aiming.

The learned-covariance arm tests the same hypothesis at zero evaluation cost:
if it also fails at 38 points, aimed local perturbations do not cross however
the aim is obtained.
