#+TITLE: The funnel crossing is done by the quench, not by the acceptance
#+OPTIONS: toc:2

All figures: 38-point Lennard-Jones, 400000 charged evaluations, one seed per
run, arms differing only in the named mechanism.

* The measurement that decides it

Every solved run's improvement ladder was dumped and the step that first reaches
the global minimum inspected. Across 22 solved runs of 32:

- *All 22 crossings arrive in a single improvement.* No run approaches the
  global minimum through a sequence of smaller gains.
- The step sizes run from 0.676 to 4.145, and the energies immediately before it
  from -169.783 to -173.252, median -172.716.
- Only 4 of 22 crossed from the icosahedral floor at -173.252378. Eight crossed
  from -173.134317 and the rest from higher.

One perturbation, one relaxation, and the chain is in the other funnel from
wherever it happened to be standing. The Monte Carlo does not walk there.

* Why that explains six failed mechanisms

Every intervention tried acts on where the chain sits or on how it accepts, and
none acts on the operator that crosses.

| mechanism                       | result                | evidence            |
|---------------------------------+-----------------------+---------------------|
| flat-histogram acceptance       | harmful               | BF 56.89 against    |
| flat below a descending cut     | harmful               | 3-5 of 24           |
| statistical temperature         | harmful               | 9 of 24             |
| well-tempered bias in energy    | null                  | 35 vs 34 of 48      |
| screen length 25 / 50 / 80      | one rate              | BF 0.16, 0.13       |
| screen length 120               | harmful               | BF 98.51            |
| merge radius 0.7 -> 1.2 -> 2.0  | harmful               | 55 -> 31 -> 24 of 72 |
| restart on stall                | null                  | 52 vs 55 of 72      |
| heavy-tailed visiting move      | null                  | 58 vs 55 of 72, BF 0.16 |

The control is Metropolis with the per-basin bias, 55 of 72.

* Two supporting facts

** Only the budget predicts the outcome

At a fixed 400000 evaluations, a 25-step screen gives 11029 hops and 551 full
relaxations; an 80-step screen gives 3782 hops and 908. Both solve 55 of 72.
Neither the number of trials nor the number of minima reached predicts success.
A search limited by how much it samples cannot be indifferent to a threefold
change in how much it samples.

** Coordinate distance cannot see a funnel

Coarsening the basin metric to funnel scale destroys the landscape before it
merges a funnel: radius 0.7 registers 365 basins and solves 55, radius 1.2
registers 46 and solves 31, radius 2.0 registers 7 and solves 24. A single
isotropic length has no setting where basins correspond to funnels.

* A statistic that looks informative and is not

Failing runs spend a median 66% of their budget after their last improvement,
which reads as a stall worth restarting from. It is not. Once a chain reaches
-173.252378, the second-lowest minimum, the only remaining event that registers
as an improvement is the global minimum itself. A run showing no improvements is
searching normally.

The data says so directly: solved runs have a nearly identical last-improvement
distribution to failed ones, median 26.3% against 33.9%. The statistic does not
separate the two populations, so no detector built on it can either, and the
restart arm measured accordingly.

This was predicted at 41 of 48 before the run on exactly that misreading. The
prediction was wrong because the premise was, and the arm landed at 52 of 72.

* What follows

The crossing is a rare event whose rate is a property of the perturbation and
the quench that follows it, and whose expected count scales with the evaluation
budget rather than with the number of trials. Interventions on the acceptance
rule, on the sampling weight, on the temperature and on the basin bias are
acting downstream of it, which is what the eight measurements above say
collectively rather than one at a time.

The leverage is in the move and the relaxation. That is where a mechanism has to
act to change this number.

The one arm that does act there was tried. The crate already carried a Tsallis
visiting distribution, unused by this driver, whose whole point is a scale-free
tail that supplies large excursions without a chosen step length. Adding it to
the library gives 58 of 72 against 55, a Bayes factor of 0.16, which is evidence
for one shared rate rather than for a gain.

That is the eighth null and the honest reading of the set is not that the next
mechanism will work. A difference of +0.04 needs on the order of eighteen
hundred seeds an arm to resolve, so an effect this size is not the thing being
looked for even if it is real. Two arms landing on exactly +3 of 72 with the
same Bayes factor is what a null looks like sampled twice, and combining them to
chase an additive gain would be mining the noise.

What the measurements support is a statement about the problem rather than a
method: at this budget the outcome is set by how many perturbation-and-quench
draws the budget buys and by the probability that one of them lands across the
funnel boundary, and that probability did not move under any reweighting of
which draws get kept.

* The structural reseed is an oracle, and the transfer test says so

The crate carries a reseed move, never wired into this driver, which keeps a
fraction of the current structure and regrows the rest with a local order drawn
from a library: the structure's own observed order, face-centred cubic,
hexagonal close packed, icosahedral, simple cubic.

Switched on at 38 points it solves *72 of 72* against a control of 55, a Bayes
factor of 38478. The global minimum of the 38-point cluster is a face-centred
cubic truncated octahedron, and face-centred cubic is in the library.

The library holds no decahedral and no tetrahedral entry, which are the
morphologies of the 75- and 98-point global minima, so those two systems test
whether the move searches or matches. At a million charged evaluations, 48 seeds:

| system | control | reseed | deepest, control | deepest, reseed |
|--------+---------+--------+------------------+-----------------|
| LJ75   |    0/48 |   0/48 |        -396.2822 |       -394.6373 |
| LJ98   |    2/48 |   0/48 |        -543.6654 |       -539.5701 |

It does not merely fail to transfer, it is worse on both systems by both
measures, losing the two solves the control finds at 98 points and reaching a
shallower energy at both sizes. The move builds the answer where the answer is
in its library and spends the budget badly where it is not.

*72 of 72 is therefore an oracle result and belongs with the Steinhardt-order
and twinning arms rather than with the measurements.* A Bayes factor of 38478 on
an arm that presupposes the answer is worth nothing, which is the reason the
transfer test was named as the deciding one before the 72 of 72 was seen rather
than after.

Adding the heavy-tailed visiting move alongside the reseed gives 58 of 72, down
from 72: an arm proposing noise takes draws from an arm proposing structure. The
consistent reading across every arm in this file is that what a proposal
contains matters and how large it is does not.

* Noise inside the quench, and why precision is load bearing

The basin of attraction of a starting point belongs to the minimiser, not to the
landscape alone, so perturbing the descent should send the same starting point
to a different minimum. That is the one factor in "perturbation then quench" no
acceptance rule, weight, temperature or bias reaches, and unlike a template
library isotropic gradient noise cannot encode an answer.

Noise scaled to a fraction `eta` of the local gradient, applied to the screening
descent only, 72 seeds:

| eta  | solved |
|------+--------|
| 0    |  55/72 |
| 0.25 |  46/72 |
| 0.50 |  43/72 |
| 1.00 |   5/72 |

Monotone and severe. At `eta = 0` the arm reproduces the control hop for hop, so
the switch is a true no-op and the gradient is the mechanism rather than an
artefact.

The reading is the opposite of the hypothesis. A sloppier minimiser does not buy
exploration, it buys nothing: the quench's precision is load bearing, and effort
diverted from converging is lost rather than spent. Together with the screen
sweep, where 120 steps is worse than 25 while 25, 50 and 80 are one rate, the
quench has an accuracy it must reach and no benefit past it.

* Regrowth from observed order, and the limit of the compactness prior

Dropping every named packing from the reseeding library leaves one arm that
regrows the cluster from the local order it already stands on, carrying nothing
external. At 38 points it solves *68 of 72* against 55, BF 19.85, difference
+0.178 [+0.069, +0.292]. It is the only non-oracle arm in this file that beats
the control.

It does not transfer. Measured where the control has room to move:

| system, budget | control | self-reseed |
|----------------+---------+-------------|
| LJ75, 1e6      |    1/72 |        0/72 |
| LJ98, 1e6      |    2/72 |        1/72 |
| LJ75, 4e6      |    4/48 |        1/48 |

Three measurements, all the same sign, none decisive alone; pooled they are
2 of 192 against 7, BF 0.36 and a difference of -0.026 [-0.060, +0.004]. The
honest reading is that the arm is mildly harmful off 38 points, and certainly
not general.

The mechanism explains both halves. Growth by coordination peel embeds a
compactness prior, which is Wulff physics rather than knowledge of any answer,
and a compact prior predicts the 38-point answer, a face-centred cubic truncated
octahedron. It does not predict a Marks decahedron, and 75 points is where it
fails. The prior transfers exactly as far as compactness predicts the
morphology, which is a bounded claim about a structural prior and not a search
method.

So the arm belongs with the oracles in what it licenses, even though it encodes
no template: it works where its prior happens to agree with the answer. The
difference from the full reseeding library is one of degree, not of kind, and
the 68 of 72 should not be reported as a general result.

* The growth family, and the order it cannot reach

Three arms build a candidate by regrowing the cluster rather than displacing it,
and all three behave the same way.

| arm                              | LJ38          | LJ75, 4e6 |
|----------------------------------+---------------+-----------|
| control                          | 55/72, 34/48  |      4/48 |
| reseed, full template library    | 72/72         |      0/48 |
| reseed from observed order only  | 68/72         |      1/48 |
| growth source under a posterior  | 48/48         |      2/48 |

Every one of them is far above the control at 38 points and below it at 75. The
posterior version is the sharpest case: it solves every seed at 38 points, so
the allocator is selecting well rather than diluting, and it still loses at 75.
Learning which arm to use cannot help when no arm builds the thing wanted.

The template set is face-centred cubic, hexagonal close packed, icosahedral and
simple cubic, and the reason decahedral is missing is not an oversight that
could be repaired by adding an entry. *Decahedral order is not local.* A Marks
decahedron is face-centred cubic tetrahedra joined across twin planes; every
first coordination shell in it is close packed, and the five-fold character
lives in the joins. There is no local order to grow from, so no growth arm
reaches it however its source is chosen.

That is the general statement the three arms measure between them: growth from
local order reaches morphologies whose defining order sits in the first
coordination shell and cannot reach morphologies defined by a global
arrangement. It also predicts which systems each arm will flatter, which is why
38 points, whose answer is a face-centred cubic truncated octahedron, reads as a
triumph and 75 points does not.

* Growth and twinning together

Growth from local order and twinning are the two kinds of structural order a
cluster has, and each alone fails on the system the other one covers. Put both
in the library and let the posterior choose:

| arm                             |         LJ38 | LJ75, 4e6 |
|---------------------------------+--------------+-----------|
| control                         | 55/72, 34/48 |      4/48 |
| growth under a posterior        |        48/48 |      2/48 |
| growth and twinning, posterior  |        48/48 |     21/48 |

At 38 points, +0.286 [+0.163, +0.422], BF 6244.62. At 75 points, +0.347
[+0.186, +0.504], BF 707.71.

The pair is what carries it, and the arms say so separately. Growth alone is
*below* the control at 75 points, at 2 of 48 against 4, because no local order
builds a Marks decahedron. Twinning is the operation that joins close-packed
fragments across a plane, which is what a decahedron is. Neither arm is
configured per system: the same library runs on both.

*No posterior is involved in these numbers, and an earlier version of this note
said there was.* Arm selection reads

: match (&context, cfg.allocate_moves) { (None, true) => allocator.select(..),
:                                        (None, false) => rng.random_range(..) }

and none of the arms above sets `allocate_moves`, so the kernel is drawn
uniformly. `learn_construction` chooses the growth *source* inside the reseed
arm and not which arm fires. What these campaigns measure is the composition of
the move library under uniform sampling. The depth-rewarded allocator sat behind
the same gate and never executed: with it nominally on, all three systems
returned results identical to the uniform runs down to the sixth decimal of the
deepest energy, which is what a flag that does nothing looks like.

That also withdraws the account given for 98 points below. Attributing 0 of 48
to a posterior failing to learn cannot be right when no posterior was
running.

That is the difference from every disqualified arm above. The full template
library solves 38 points by carrying the answer's packing and is worse
elsewhere; this library carries a structural vocabulary rather than a
morphology, and improves both systems at once without being told which.

** And it fails on the third morphology

98 points, whose global minimum is a tetrahedral face-centred cubic fragment,
at four million evaluations and 48 seeds: the control solves 5 and the pair
solves *0*, reaching -541.895 against -543.665. Difference -0.102
[-0.206, -0.023], BF 4.21 against.

So the pair is not general either. It improves two morphologies and destroys the
third, and the third is the one whose answer growth alone should suit.

The mechanism is arm dilution, which is the prediction that was made before this
campaign and that 38 and 75 points appeared to refute. A posterior over arms
needs successes to learn from, and at 98 points the control succeeds in 5 runs of
48. Over a single run there are almost no improvement events to allocate on, so
the twin arm is never switched off, and it takes draws from the growth arm that
the system actually needs. Where the control succeeds often, as at 38 and 75
points, the posterior has signal and selects well; where it succeeds rarely the
posterior is inert and the extra arm is pure cost.

That also explains why the effect looked general across two systems: both had
enough successes for the allocator to work with. Generality of the vocabulary
and generality of the *learning* are different claims, and only the first
survives.


* Twinning is an oracle, and that closes the structural line

A decahedron *is* a twinned structure: face-centred cubic tetrahedra joined
across twin planes. A move that mirrors a structure across one of its dense
planes is therefore the construction of the answer at 75 points, in the same way
that a face-centred cubic template is the construction of the answer at 38. The
75-point result of 21 of 48 against 4 is withdrawn on that ground and the growth
and twinning line is closed.

That decides the whole structural family the same way, and the pattern is worth
stating plainly because it is the session's result:

- Every move that measurably improves a system encodes the morphology of that
  system's answer. Face-centred cubic growth at 38 points, twinning at 75.
- Every move that encodes no morphology measures at the control. Heavy-tailed
  visiting steps, restarts, quench noise, and every acceptance-level and
  bias-level mechanism in the table above.
- Regrowth from observed order sits between the two and fails for the same
  reason: its compactness prior is a weak morphological commitment, so it helps
  at 38 points and hurts at 75 and 98.

So on this landscape the leverage that exists lives in move design, and move
design that works is chemistry knowledge about the target rather than search.
That is a statement about where basin hopping's power on clusters actually comes
from, and it is the opposite of the premise that better statistics on the
acceptance step is what has been missing.

* The composed excursion, and the first non-oracle gains on the hard systems

The burst arm composes a geometric number of surface relocations into one
proposal, so an excursion pays a single acceptance test instead of the product
of its intermediates. Measured against controls that replicate across two
machines exactly (55/72, 4/48, 5/48):

| system | control | burst | BF |
|--------+---------+-------+------|
| LJ38   |   55/72 | 48/72 | 0.31 |
| LJ75   |    4/48 | 14/48 | 5.78 |
| LJ98   |    5/48 | 11/48 | 0.64 |

At 75 points the difference is +0.204 [+0.055, +0.355], BF 5.78: the first
non-oracle arm to beat a control decisively anywhere. The direction of the
trade matches the mechanism: the small system's crossings need short
excursions and lose a little to burst overhead; the large systems need long
excursions that Metropolis kills as separate hops, and composing them is worth
a factor of three.

The depth-rewarded Thompson bandit is the other live positive (64/72, 10/48,
7/48; every sign positive, no single BF decisive; confirmation seeds running).
Nested search as configured is refuted: 12/72, 0/48, 3/48.

* The unexploited factor: move locality in the quench

Every measured-productive move changes one atom. Every trial nonetheless pays
a full-system screening relaxation at full-evaluation prices: 33 charged per
hop, dominated by descending coordinates the move never touched. A
single-particle move's energy change is n pair terms against n^2/2 for the
full system, so the honest price of a frozen-environment descent of the moved
atom alone is about 2/n of an evaluation; even charged at the full unit rate
it filters trials an order of magnitude cheaper than the current screen.

Since the budget is the only variable that predicts success, this converts
directly: a local pre-screen that relaxes the moved atom in the frozen field,
promotes survivors to the ordinary screen, and charges conservatively at full
rate, multiplies the draws the same budget buys. The design is standard
Monte Carlo economics (incremental evaluation) applied to the quenched
landscape, where it has not been: the screen, the burst composition and the
one-atom completion all act on the same few coordinates.

Implementation: the objective lives with the caller, so the local descent
enters as a callback beside `relax` (a `settle` closure: coordinates of one
atom, frozen environment, few iterations), used by the driver's screen path
for the arms whose moves are local. Charged at the full unit rate first, so
any win is understated rather than manufactured; fractional charging is a
later refinement with its own audit.

* The composite holds on all three

burst + depth-rewarded Thompson, one configuration, nothing per system:
LJ38 55/72 against control 55/72; LJ75 13/48 against 4/48; LJ98 16/48
against 5/48. The bandit recovers burst's small-system deficit entirely and
extends its large-system gain. Confirmation seeds: tdep alone confirms at 38
points (115/144 against 101/144) and is null on the hard systems, so the
allocation pays exactly when the library holds an arm worth allocating to,
which is the composed excursion.

* Tempering over the composite, and the archive ratchet refuted

Four replicas exchanging over the composed-excursion stack, one budget, one
configuration across systems:

| system | control | comb  | PT over comb | BF vs control |
|--------+---------+-------+--------------+---------------|
| LJ38   |   55/72 | 55/72 |        51/72 |             - |
| LJ75   |    4/48 | 13/48 |        16/48 |         19.61 |
| LJ98   |    5/48 | 16/48 |        21/48 |        202.41 |

+0.327 [+0.161, +0.487] at 98 points. Against comb alone the addition is
directionally positive and unconfirmed (BF 0.28), and the rung-bias variant is
worse everywhere, so the exchange is carrying replicas, not the bias. The
trade has burst's shape again: sustained excursions pay on the hard
landscapes and cost a little on the easy one. The earlier tempering null was
measured over Metropolis walkers with the old library; the mechanism the
replicas needed to amplify did not exist yet.

The archive ratchet is refuted as built: 4/72, 0/48, 2/48. Thompson over
per-site discovery posteriors explores the network breadth-first, and
discovering new arrangements is cheap everywhere, so the acquisition never
concentrates. The lesson matches the flat-histogram failure: rewarding
discovery of states, like rewarding residence at energies, spends the budget
on abundance. What pays, in every positive result in this file, is spending
it on descent while removing the tax on excursions.

* Tabu composes; the early-gated screen does not

Tabu over the composite, paired at 72 seeds against comb on the same seeds:
56/72 against 55 at 38 points, 20/72 against 25 at 75, and *40/72 against 20*
at 98, the strongest number measured there. Hard exclusion of the funnel the
chain stalls in composes with the excursion stack exactly where revisiting
is the failure mode. Tabu alone: 53/72, 5/72, 17/72.

The early-terminated screen is refuted with this stack: comb with early
falls to 24/72 at 38 points against 55 without it, so the gating the staged
quench needs cannot be this gate. The settle-plus-early interaction at 75
points (28/72 against 3) says the pieces interact strongly and the staged
premise remains open rather than closed.
