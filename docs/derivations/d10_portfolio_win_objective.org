* D10: Portfolio win-objective endgame (budget conversion)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: d10-portfolio-win-objective
:END:

** Scope
This is the *operating algorithm* that converts remaining budget from
exploration to polish. It composes:
- D5 geometric polish conversion \(P_{\mathrm{conv}}(p)\),
- D9 discovery value \(\theta_{\mathrm{disc}}\) (or D4 Beta fallback),
- the explore-first schedule form of D5.

It is the mathematical content of the paper's Algorithm (budget-only
portfolio) lines ``raise \(n_{\mathrm{res}}\) / spend reserved polish'',
productized as the one-step-lookahead rule in =portfolio.rs=.

** Two-basin win model
Remaining work \(R\). Split into polish work \(p\in\{0,\ldots,R\}\) and
exploration slices \(e=\lfloor(R-p)/s\rfloor\) of slice size \(s\).

- With probability \(q_0\), the incumbent already holds the best basin among
  those reachable by further exploration; then only polish matters.
- With probability \(1-q_0\), exploration can still improve; each of \(e\)
  independent slices finds a better basin with probability \(\theta\), so the
  probability of *no* improvement is \((1-\theta)^{e}\).

Under D9, \(\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{disc}}\) and \(q_0=1-\theta_{\mathrm{disc}}\).
Under D4 fallback, \(\theta\) is the aggregated Beta mean and
\(q_0=\beta/(\alpha+\beta)\), with
\(\mathbb{E}[(1-\theta)^{e}]=\prod_{i<e}(\beta+i)/(\alpha+\beta+i)\).

Let \(P_{\mathrm{conv}}(p)\) be the posterior probability that \(p\) polish work
units close the near-best gap (D5 / NIG contraction posterior).

** Win objective
\begin{equation}
W(p)
  =
  \Bigl(
    q_0 + (1-q_0)\bigl(1-\pi_e\bigr)
  \Bigr)
  \,
  P_{\mathrm{conv}}(p),
\tag{D10.1}
\end{equation}
where \(\pi_e=(1-\theta)^{e}\) (D9) or the Beta product (D4).

*** Proposition D10.1 (explore-first structure)
Among schedules that spend exactly \(e\) exploration slices and \(p\) polish
work units, any schedule that interleaves polish before the last successful
exploration is weakly dominated by explore-then-polish (D5 exchange). Hence
it is enough to optimize \(W(p)\) over the scalar reserve \(p\).

*** Proposition D10.2 (one-step-lookahead endgame)
For the monotone stopping problem of choosing whether to enter the polish
phase now, the rule
\begin{equation}
\text{endgame now}
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  p^{*}(R) = R
  \quad\text{where}\quad
  p^{*}(R)=\arg\max_{0\le p\le R} W(p)
\tag{D10.2}
\end{equation}
is optimal in the Chow–Robbins sense for this one-step-lookahead model
(maximize expected win, monotone value of remaining exploration). In code,
\(W\) is maximized on a 12-point grid of \(p\); endgame starts when the
maximizer takes the entire remaining budget, subject to the D5 asymmetric
reserve floor \(R\le\max(B/4,\,p_{\mathrm{quantile}})\).

** Algorithm D10 (budget conversion)
Input: remaining \(R\), slice \(s\), discovery \(\theta\) or Beta \((\alpha,\beta)\),
contraction posterior, total budget \(B\).
1. Compute reserve floor \(r_{\lfloor}= \mathrm{clamp}(p_{\mathrm{quantile}}, B/4, B/3)\).
2. If \(R\le r_{\lfloor}\), enter endgame polish immediately.
3. Else evaluate \(W(p)\) on a grid \(p_k=k R/K\), \(k=0,\ldots,K\) (default \(K=12\)).
4. If \(\arg\max_p W(p)=R\), enter endgame; else allocate one exploration slice
   by floored Thompson / TPE / regime router and loop.

** Executable checks
=proofs/d10_portfolio_win_objective.py=:
- Identity \(q_0=1-\theta\) under D9.
- \(\pi_e=(1-\theta)^{e}\) and Beta product formulas.
- On a fixed \((\theta,P_{\mathrm{conv}})\), explore-first \(W(p)\) is unimodal or
  has maximizer at boundary consistent with monotone conversion.
- Grid argmax matches exhaustive evaluation on small \(R\).
