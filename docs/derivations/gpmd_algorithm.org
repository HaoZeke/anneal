#+TITLE: GPMD: Gap-proportional Metropolis (local driver note)
#+SUBTITLE: D6 packaging for a shipped arm — not a research algorithm
#+OPTIONS: toc:2

* (I) Understand the problem

** What is the unknown?
A temperature law \(T=T(x)\) (and a consistent proposal-scale adaptation rule)
for a Metropolis chain so that, on a *local* quadratic basin, the expected
one-step decrease of the *current chain-state energy* is positive and near
the model optimum under evolution-strategy proposal scaling.

** What are the data?
- Local model objective \(f(x)=\|x\|^2/2\) measured from the basin bottom
  (sphere). On a general basin one substitutes a local quadratic; see D6
  anisotropic remarks.
- Proposal \(y=x+\sigma z\) with \(z\sim N(0,I_d)\) and ES scale
  \(\sigma=c\|x\|/d\) (Rechenberg).
- Metropolis acceptance \(a_T(\Delta)=\min\{1,\exp(-\Delta/T)\}\),
  \(\Delta=f(y)-f(x)\).
- Work-unit budget \(B\).

** What is the condition?
Temperature must follow from the sign of the state-gain functional in the
limiting model (not a free geometric schedule). The unknown is *local
descent of the chain state*, not global multimodal escape and not
best-so-far one-step improvement (the latter is independent of \(T\);
see D6 §Scope).

** Literature parent
- *Parent mathematical model:* D6 (annealed Metropolis state-drift scaling)
  in =docs/derivations/d6_annealed_descent_scaling.org= /
  =proofs/d6_annealed_descent_scaling.py=.
- *Engineering parents:* adaptive Metropolis for covariance shape
  (Haario–Saksman–Tamminen); Robbins–Monro for scale.
- *Not claimed:* Xiang dual annealing / GSA visiting as a global solver.

* (II) Devise a plan

** Related problems
- Maximize \((1+1)\)-ES progress on the sphere (Rechenberg): \(T=0\) limit of Metropolis.
- Stationary-chain optimal scaling (Roberts–Gelman–Gilks): different objective
  (diffusion speed of the equilibrium chain), not state descent under budget.

** Plan
1. Expand \(\Delta\) exactly for \(f=\|x\|^2/2\).
2. Normalize by \(f/d\), impose ES scaling, pass to the \(d\to\infty\) limit to
   obtain a Gaussian law for \(D\) (model M1; same as D6.2).
3. Define normalized gain \(G(c,\theta)\) (D6.3).
4. Prove the density-ratio identity (I1 / D6.6) and the pairing formula (I2 / D6.7).
5. Conclude the critical window (T1 / Prop. D6.1).
6. Cross-check the small-step expansion (D6.8).
7. Choose an interior operating point \(\theta_\star=\tfrac12\) using the
   numerically maximized residual rate \(G^\star(\theta)/G^\star(0)\).
8. Invert \(\theta=Td/f\) with the implementable proxy \(f_{\mathrm{best}}\) for
   the unknown basin bottom \(f^\star\), yielding the gap-proportional law (A1).
9. Write the explicit algorithm and check it (SymPy, Lean algebra, unit tests).

* (III) Carry out the plan

** Exact energy increment
Let \(f(x)=\|x\|^2/2\) and \(y=x+\sigma z\). Then
\begin{equation}
\Delta
  = f(y)-f(x)
  = \sigma\, x^\top z + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\|z\|^2.
\tag{GPMD.1}
\end{equation}
(This is an identity, not a limit.)

** ES scaling and normalization
Set \(\sigma=c\|x\|/d\) with \(c>0\) fixed and write
\[
D := \frac{d\,\Delta}{f(x)}.
\]
Since \(f(x)=\|x\|^2/2\),
\begin{equation}
D
  = \frac{d}{f(x)}\Bigl(\sigma\, x^\top z + \tfrac12\sigma^2\|z\|^2\Bigr)
  = 2c\,\frac{x^\top z}{\|x\|}
    + c^2\,\frac{\|z\|^2}{d}.
\tag{GPMD.2}
\end{equation}
As \(d\to\infty\): \(\|z\|^2/d\to 1\) in probability, and
\(x^\top z/\|x\|\sim N(0,1)\), so
\begin{equation}
D \;\Longrightarrow\; N(c^2,\,4c^2).
\tag{M1 / D6.2}
\end{equation}
(M1) is an *asymptotic model*. All identities below are exact *inside* that
model (same convention as D6). Finite-\(d\) corrections are not claimed.

** Gain functional
With dimensionless temperature \(\theta=Td/f(x)\),
\begin{equation}
G(c,\theta)
  = -\mathbb{E}\bigl[D\min\{1,e^{-D/\theta}\}\bigr].
\tag{GPMD.3 / D6.3}
\end{equation}
This is the expected *normalized* decrease of the *current* energy, not of
the incumbent record (D6 §Scope).

** Small-step expansion (independent check)
For small \(c\), expand (GPMD.3) in the limiting Gaussian model to obtain
\begin{equation}
G(c,\theta)
  = c^2\frac{2-\theta}{\theta}+O(c^3)
  \qquad(\theta>0).
\tag{GPMD.4 / D6.8}
\end{equation}
The leading coefficient changes sign at \(\theta=2\), consistent with (T1)
below, but the global proof does *not* use the expansion (same warning as D6).

** Identity (I1) — Gaussian density ratio (D6.6)
Let \(p_c\) be the density of \(N(c^2,4c^2)\). For \(u>0\),
\begin{equation}
\log\frac{p_c(-u)}{p_c(u)}
  = -\frac{(-u-c^2)^2-(u-c^2)^2}{2\cdot(4c^2)}
  = -\frac{u}{2}.
\tag{I1 / D6.6}
\end{equation}

*Proof (full algebra).* For any Gaussian \(N(\mu,\sigma^2)\) with density \(p\),
\[
\log p(-u)-\log p(u)
  = -\frac{(-u-\mu)^2-(u-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
Expand the numerator:
\[
(-u-\mu)^2-(u-\mu)^2
  = (u^2+2u\mu+\mu^2)-(u^2-2u\mu+\mu^2)
  = 4u\mu,
\]
so
\[
\log p(-u)-\log p(u)
  = -\frac{4u\mu}{2\sigma^2}
  = -\frac{2u\mu}{\sigma^2}.
\]
Specialize \(\mu=c^2\), \(\sigma^2=4c^2\) (\(c\neq 0\)):
\[
-\frac{2u c^2}{4c^2} = -\frac{u}{2}.
\]
Exponentiating gives \(p_c(-u)/p_c(u)=e^{-u/2}\). \(\square\)

(Lean checks the algebraic steps of this expansion and the specialization
\(-2\mu/\sigma^2=-1/2\) when \(\sigma^2=4\mu\). SymPy re-derives the same
identities symbolically.)

** Proposition (T1 / D6.1) — critical temperature in the limiting model
For every \(c>0\), \(G(c,\theta)>0\) when \(0<\theta<2\), \(G(c,2)=0\), and
\(G(c,\theta)<0\) when \(\theta>2\).

*Proof (pairing, as D6.1).* Write \(G=-\mathbb{E}[D\,a_\theta(D)]\) with
\(a_\theta(D)=\min\{1,e^{-D/\theta}\}\). Split the expectation into \(D>0\) and
\(D<0\). On \(\{D\le 0\}\) one has \(a_\theta=1\). On \(\{D>0\}\) one has
\(a_\theta=e^{-D/\theta}\). Pair \(+u\) and \(-u\) for \(u>0\): the contribution of
the pair is proportional to
\[
u\,p_c(u)\,e^{-u/\theta}
  - (-u)\,p_c(-u)
  = u\,p_c(u)\Bigl(
      e^{-u/\theta}
      + \frac{p_c(-u)}{p_c(u)}
    \Bigr)
  - 2u\,p_c(-u)
\]
more cleanly, following D6.7: using \(p_c(-u)=e^{-u/2}p_c(u)\) from (I1),
\begin{equation}
G(c,\theta)
  = \int_0^\infty u\,p_c(u)\,
    \bigl(e^{-u/2}-e^{-u/\theta}\bigr)\,du.
\tag{I2 / D6.7}
\end{equation}
Here \(u\,p_c(u)>0\) for all \(u>0\). For fixed \(u>0\) and \(\theta>0\),
\[
e^{-u/2}>e^{-u/\theta}
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  -\tfrac12 > -\tfrac1\theta
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \tfrac1\theta > \tfrac12
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \theta < 2,
\]
with equality at \(\theta=2\) and the reverse inequality for \(\theta>2\).
Therefore the integrand is pointwise positive / zero / negative exactly when
\(\theta\) is less than / equal to / greater than \(2\), which yields (T1).
\(\square\)

** Choice of operating point \(\theta_\star=\tfrac12\)
Any \(\theta\in(0,2)\) is feasible for local descent. We fix the interior
value
\[
\theta_\star := \tfrac12
\]
because:
1. It lies strictly inside the window of (T1) (Lean: \(\theta_\star\in(0,2)\)).
2. Numerical maximization of \(G(c,\theta_\star)\) over \(c>0\) (SymPy/MC in
   =proofs/gpmd_derive.py=; same constants as D6) yields
   \(c^\star\approx 1.32\), \(G^\star(\theta_\star)\approx 0.368\), and
   acceptance \(\alpha^\star\approx 0.32\).
3. Relative to the pure-descent maximum \(G^\star(0)\approx 0.405\) (Rechenberg
   limit recovered as \(\theta\to 0\)), one has
   \[
   \frac{G^\star(\theta_\star)}{G^\star(0)}
     \approx \frac{0.368}{0.405}
     \approx 0.91.
   \]
   So \(\theta_\star=\tfrac12\) keeps about *91% of the maximal model descent
   rate* while leaving a non-trivial Metropolis temperature (acceptance
   rises from \(\approx 0.27\) at \(\theta=0\) to \(\approx 0.32\)).

** Proxy for the basin bottom: \(f^\star\) vs \(f_{\mathrm{best}}\)
In the sphere model, \(\theta=Td/f(x)\) uses \(f(x)=\|x\|^2/2\) measured from
the *true* bottom \(f^\star=0\). In a general basin one wants
\[
\theta = \frac{T\,d}{f(x)-f^\star},
\]
but \(f^\star\) is unknown. The implementable gap is the best-so-far record
\(f_{\mathrm{best}}\le f(x)\) maintained by the chain. Then
\begin{equation}
T(x)
  = \theta_\star\frac{f(x)-f_{\mathrm{best}}}{d}
  = \frac12\frac{f(x)-f_{\mathrm{best}}}{d}
\tag{A1}
\end{equation}
is exactly the inversion of \(\theta=\theta_\star\) with the *proxy gap*
\(f-f_{\mathrm{best}}\) in place of \(f-f^\star\).

Justification of the proxy:
- Always \(f_{\mathrm{best}}\ge f^\star\), so \(f-f_{\mathrm{best}}\le f-f^\star\): the
  implemented temperature is *no hotter* than the model law with the true
  bottom. That keeps the chain on the *cold* side of the window when the
  record is imperfect—safer for local descent than overheating.
- As the record approaches the basin floor, \(f_{\mathrm{best}}\downarrow f^\star\)
  and (A1) recovers the model law.
- When \(f=f_{\mathrm{best}}\), (A1) forces \(T\to 0^+\) (with a numerical floor
  \(\varepsilon\)), i.e. pure descent at the incumbent—consistent with
  “no thermal exploration needed when the chain sits at the best known
  point of the current basin.”

** Proposal shape and size (implementation of the free \(c\))
The model free parameter \(c\) is realized as a *scale* multiplier on a
proposal covariance: isotropic until enough samples accumulate, then a
diagonal / scatter estimate (Haario-style running mean and outer products),
with Robbins–Monro adaptation of \(\log\sigma\) targeting \(\alpha^\star=0.32\).
Shape adaptation is justified by D6’s anisotropic remark: isotropic proposals
on ill-conditioned quadratics collapse the critical temperature; whitening
restores \(\theta_c=2\).

** Budget split
Reserve a polish fraction \(\rho_{\mathrm{pol}}=\tfrac14\) of \(B\) for a terminal
projected-gradient (or coordinate) polish of \(x_{\mathrm{best}}\). The fraction
is a fixed design constant of the algorithm (same spirit as D5’s polish
reserve), not a free cooling parameter.

** Algorithm GPMD (explicit)

1. Initialize \(x\) (given \(x_0\) or random in the box); evaluate
   \(f\leftarrow f(x)\); set \(f_{\mathrm{best}}\leftarrow f\), \(x_{\mathrm{best}}\leftarrow x\).
2. Set \(B_{\mathrm{pol}}\leftarrow\lfloor B/4\rfloor\) and
   \(B_{\mathrm{sa}}\leftarrow B-B_{\mathrm{pol}}\).
3. While work \(<B_{\mathrm{sa}}\):
   a. \(T\leftarrow\tfrac12\max(f-f_{\mathrm{best}},\varepsilon)/d\)    (A1).
   b. Propose \(y\sim N(x,\sigma^2\hat\Sigma)\) (isotropic if scatter thin);
      reflect into the box.
   c. Metropolis accept/reject at temperature \(T\); charge one objective eval.
   d. Update scatter and Robbins–Monro \(\log\sigma\) toward \(\alpha^\star=0.32\);
      if accepted and \(f<f_{\mathrm{best}}\), update the record.
4. Spend the polish reserve on projected gradient (if a gradient is supplied)
   or coordinate polish of \(x_{\mathrm{best}}\).
5. Return \(x_{\mathrm{best}}\).

Parameters introduced by the math: \(\theta_\star=\tfrac12\),
\(\alpha^\star\approx 0.32\) (from maximizing \(G\) at \(\theta_\star\)),
\(\rho_{\mathrm{pol}}=\tfrac14\), gap floor \(\varepsilon>0\). No free geometric
cooling schedule.
