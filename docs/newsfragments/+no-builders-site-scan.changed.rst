The scan that seats the atoms of a symmetry completion on their sites now tests whole sites four at a time: the reach on the first coordinate, the squared distance against the limit the scalar test allows, and the root against the cutoff, in the arithmetic of the scalar test. It used to clear sites on two coordinates and call the scalar test on about three sites per image, which on 38 to 55 points was most of the completion. A plain Lennard-Jones 38-point run spends about 5 to 7 percent less time outside the potential, and the completion it returns is bit for bit the one before.
