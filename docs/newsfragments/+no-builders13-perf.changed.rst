The hop that steps a cluster away from its own descriptors takes about a third of the instructions, and the step is the same bits. The stacked SOAP and ACE Jacobian is no longer formed as three dense matrices of 3N columns and then scanned for its nonzeros: one pass over the neighbours of each centre builds that centre's rows as a compact block, which is summed into the normal equations and dropped, and the Cholesky solve works on a flat matrix. The real harmonics keep their Legendre table and phases on the stack and read their normalisations from a table. On a binary cluster of 115 atoms a step costs 1.40G instructions (0.48G now) on blj115n38, and 2.63G (0.86G now) on blj115n55, per step averaged over a 100000-call walk. The step before and after is bit for bit the same, with and without species and with frozen atoms, and the dense route stays for a non-finite structure.
