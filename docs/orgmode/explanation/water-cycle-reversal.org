#+begin_export rst
=============================
Hydrogen-bond cycle reversal
=============================
#+end_export

This page explains why the rigid water search reverses cycles of hydrogen bonds.
Its water model is the transferable intermolecular potential with four points (TIP4P).
It covers how the move works, how it fits with the other moves, and what it changed on a fixed set of clusters.

* The other moves

A rigid water has a centre and an orientation, six coordinates per molecule.
=Config::for_tip4p= follows Wales and Hodges (1998) with two moves.
=rtrans= shifts every centre by a small random step.
=rrot= adds a small random increment to every rotation.
After each move the search relaxes the cluster to a local minimum.
It then accepts or rejects that minimum by its energy.
A move allocator picks the move for each hop.
It favours the moves that have paid off on this run.

* Why bond direction matters

In a water cluster each hydrogen bond has a donor and an acceptor.
The donor points one of its hydrogens at the acceptor's oxygen.
Two clusters can put every oxygen in the same place and still differ in which way the bonds point.
Their energies differ, because the charges sit in different places.

The molecules around a ring of bonds must all turn at once to change one arrangement into another.
A move that turns one molecule breaks its bonds.
The relaxation can then turn it straight back to the minimum it left.
=rrot= turns every molecule, but each in a random direction.
That rarely gives a whole ring that points the other way.

* The move

=rcycle= reverses one directed cycle of bonds.
It starts at a random molecule that donates a bond.
It follows a random donated bond to the next molecule, and repeats.
It never steps straight back to the molecule it came from.
It stops when it reaches a molecule it has already visited.
The molecules from that visit on form a cycle of three or more.
Two waters that donate to each other would keep the same two bonds when both turn, so the walk skips that pair.

Each molecule on the cycle then turns about its own oxygen.
The turn maps the plane of its two hydrogens onto the plane of its other hydrogen and the oxygen of the molecule before it on the cycle.
Its donating hydrogen ends near that oxygen, and its other hydrogen moves a little.
Each molecule on the cycle still donates one bond and accepts one along the cycle, now the other way round.
The oxygens do not move, so the network stays.
The new hydrogen positions are close to the bonded ones, and the relaxation settles them.

The idea is the loop move for proton arrangements in ice, from Rahman and Stillinger (1972).
In ice that move is used to sample arrangements at equilibrium.
Here the walk is not uniform over cycles, so the move has no proposal ratio.
It is a move for finding minima, not for sampling.

The allocator sees =rcycle= as a third move, beside =rtrans= and =rrot=.
If the walk finds no cycle, the move returns the cluster unchanged.
The search then draws another move for that hop, so it spends no quench on an unchanged cluster.
It has no rate to set.
The allocator gives it more hops when it pays off on that run and fewer when it does not.

* What it changed

The comparison runs =Config::recommended_tip4p= with and without the move.
It uses nine sizes from 10 to 21 molecules and 32 seeds per size, seeds 35000 to 35031.
These seeds were chosen after the move was frozen, and each run ran once.
Each run has 500,000 calls into the potential and starts from the same recorded start.
A run is a hit when its lowest energy comes within 0.01 kJ/mol of the Cambridge Cluster Database value.

With the move the search hits 46 of 288 runs, against 34 without it.
Paired by size and seed, 12 pairs hit only with the move and none hit only without it.
A two-sided sign test on those pairs gives p = 0.00049.
Most of the gain is at 12 molecules, with 12 hits against 2.
From 16 to 21 molecules the hits go from 0 to 2 of 192.
At every size from 12 to 21 the median lowest energy is lower with the move.

The 12-molecule gap hides a trap.
Five runs with the move and five without it end 13.591445 kJ/mol above the Cambridge value.
That minimum has 18 hydrogen bonds, against 20 in the lowest one, and its oxygens form a different network.
A cycle reversal leaves the oxygens where they are, so on its own it cannot turn one network into the other.

* Development seeds

Two earlier sets of seeds are development seeds and not a test.
Seeds 100 to 115 checked the move while it was written.
Seeds 11000 to 11031 were scored on an earlier walk that could step straight back, before the final walk was frozen.
Both sets were looked at before the walk was frozen, so this page gives no sign test for them.
