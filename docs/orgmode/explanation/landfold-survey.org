#+TITLE: Landscape embeddings for minima books
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil todo:nil num:nil author:nil ^:nil
#+STARTUP: content

This note surveys methods that place the minima of a global-optimisation run on a plane.
It picks one for =anneal landfold= and records why.
The benchmark numbers at the end come from the asv suite of the anneal_repro workflow.

* The object to map

A minima book is the list of distinct local minima that a search found.
Each entry has an energy and 3N coordinates.
anneal reads =.min= dumps, one minimum per line, and multi-frame =.xyz= files.

Each minimum becomes a DECAF row: the fraction of its atoms in each packing class of one shared codebook.
The codebook comes from anneal's own SOAP power spectrum, so the rows are histograms.
Two minima are compared by the L1 distance of their rows.
That distance is not Euclidean, and many minima share a row exactly.

The map has to do three things for a figure of an energy landscape.

1. Keep funnels and structural families apart.
   LJ38 has an fcc funnel and an icosahedral funnel [[doye1999b]].
   LJ75 and LJ98 have decahedral and tetrahedral global minima far from the icosahedral bulk [[doye1999a]] [[leary1999]].
2. Keep near neighbours together inside a funnel, so that colour by energy shows a gradient and not noise.
3. Accept minima found later without moving the old ones.
   A book grows as the search runs, and a figure series should not reshuffle.

Practical requirements follow from the use.
The input is a distance, not a Euclidean frame.
A seed must fix the map bit for bit, at any thread count.
A book of ten thousand minima must fold in seconds, so the cost has to grow like n m for m landmarks, not like n squared.

The previous paper figures used scikit-learn metric MDS on a switched L1 matrix, 1 - 1/(1 + (d/sigma)^2) with sigma the median distance.
This note calls that the Ceriotti kernel pipeline, after the sigmoid of sketch-map.
It is dense in n and has no out-of-sample step.

* Methods

** Classical and metric MDS

Classical MDS double-centres the squared distances and keeps the top two eigenvectors [[torgerson1952]].
It is deterministic up to sign and costs one dense eigensolve.
Metric and non-metric MDS minimise a stress instead [[kruskal1964]].
SMACOF is the usual solver [[borg2005]].

All of them weight every pair alike.
Distances between funnels are large and uninformative beyond "far", yet they dominate the stress.
The map then spends its area on the gaps and crushes each funnel.
Classical MDS remains the best start for anything that follows, because it is unique and cheap on a few hundred landmarks.

** Landmark MDS and Nystrom extensions

Landmark MDS embeds m landmarks by classical MDS and places every other point by a linear triangulation from its landmark distances [[desilva2003]].
Platt showed that it is a Nystrom method [[platt2005]].
Bengio and co-workers gave the matching out-of-sample formula for the spectral family [[bengio2004]].
The cost is O(n m).
The weakness is the same uniform pair weighting as classical MDS.

** Isomap, kernel PCA and Laplacian eigenmaps

Isomap runs classical MDS on graph geodesics over a k-nearest-neighbour graph [[tenenbaum2000]].
Kernel PCA diagonalises a kernel matrix [[scholkopf1998]], and Laplacian eigenmaps use the graph Laplacian [[belkin2003]].

A minima book is not a sampled manifold.
It is a set of clusters, often disconnected at small k.
Isomap then fails on disconnected graphs or joins funnels through one short edge.
Duplicate DECAF rows make the neighbour graph degenerate.

** t-SNE

t-SNE matches neighbour probabilities with a heavy-tailed kernel on the plane [[vandermaaten2008]].
Barnes-Hut [[vandermaaten2014]] and FFT interpolation [[linderman2019]] bring the cost near O(n log n).
openTSNE implements both and can add points to a fixed map [[policar2024]].

t-SNE keeps neighbourhoods very well.
It does not keep the distance between clusters, so the gap between two funnels on the plot means nothing.
Results depend on initialisation and perplexity [[kobak2019]].

** UMAP

UMAP builds a fuzzy neighbour graph and lays it out with attraction and repulsion [[mcinnes2018a]] [[mcinnes2018b]].
It is fast and keeps more of the global layout than t-SNE.
Its layout step is stochastic and only fixed by a seed in single-threaded mode.
Between-cluster distances again carry little meaning.

** PHATE

PHATE diffuses on a neighbour graph and embeds potential distances by MDS [[moon2019]].
It is built for continuous trajectories, such as cells along a lineage.
On a book of separate funnels its diffusion has little to follow.
With a precomputed distance, as here, the cost is dense.

** Sketch-map

Sketch-map passes both the high-dimensional distance D and the map distance d through sigmoids, s_D and s_d, and minimises the weighted squared difference [[ceriotti2011]].
Pairs much closer than sigma all read as zero.
Pairs much farther all read as one.
Only the middle range shapes the map.

For an energy landscape this is the right bias.
Small differences between near-identical minima fall below sigma and are ignored.
The gap between funnels saturates, so funnels separate without consuming the plot.
The original method uses farthest-point landmarks, iterative metric MDS as a start, a global grid search per point, and a per-point fit for out-of-sample placement [[ceriotti2013]].
It was later used to bias simulations in sketch-map coordinates [[tribello2012]].

Sketch-map and its variants are the common choice for structure maps in materials chemistry [[de2016]] [[cheng2020]].
These maps usually rest on SOAP [[bartok2013]], and DECAF rows are built on SOAP too.
The reference code [[sketchmapcode]] is serial C++ with conjugate gradients.

** Embeddings built for structure search

SHEAP embeds AIRSS structure-search results with a t-SNE or UMAP cost and hard-sphere repulsion between points [[shires2021]].
It shares the problems of those costs for funnel distances.
EncoderMap trains an autoencoder with a sketch-map loss [[lemke2019]].
A network adds training cost and run-to-run variance for no gain on books of this size.

** Disconnectivity graphs and metric layouts

Disconnectivity graphs plot minima by energy and join them where the lowest connecting saddle allows [[becker1997]] [[wales1998]] [[wales2003]].
Free energy variants exist [[krivov2002]].
Metric disconnectivity graphs put an order parameter on the horizontal axis [[smeeton2014]].

All of these need the saddle network.
A minima book from basin hopping [[wales1997]] has no saddles.
A layout that uses them is the right next step once anneal records saddles.
It cannot be built from a book alone.

* Choice

Landmark sketch-map is the best fit for minima books.
The sigmoid gives the two scales a landscape figure needs.
Landmarks bring the cost to O(n m).
The per-point fit places new minima without moving the old ones.
Every step can run deterministically in parallel.

=anneal landfold= implements it as follows.

1. Farthest-point landmarks [[gonzalez1985]] from a seeded first index.
   Each landmark is weighted by its Voronoi population to the power 0.5.
2. Classical MDS on the landmark distances.
   A block subspace iteration with Rayleigh-Ritz gives lambda1 and lambda2 of the double-centred Gram matrix.
3. The mixed stress of the reference code over landmark pairs.
   Stages step the weight of the raw distance term from 1 to 0.
   Each stage with a sigmoid term sweeps every landmark over a 21 by 21 grid.
   L-BFGS then relaxes all of them.
4. Each other minimum is placed by L-BFGS on its own stress against the fixed landmarks.
   It starts from the best of its nearest landmarks and their weighted centroid.

Defaults are 400 landmarks, sigma half the median landmark distance, and switch exponents (2, 4) on both sides.
These were chosen on the LJ books below, so they may favour them.
Parallel loops write disjoint slots and reduce in index order.
A seed therefore fixes the map at any thread count.

anneal already had =methods::sketchmap::SketchMap2d= for collective variables in a Euclidean frame.
=landfold= is the book pipeline: L1 on DECAF rows, the mixed stress, the grid, the weights, and a stable CLI and Python API.

* Benchmark

Books are not taken from the paper data, which was not reachable.
They are LJ38, LJ75 and LJ98 books of 2000 distinct minima each, written by the anneal example =landfold_lj_book=.
Every method maps one set of DECAF rows with the L1 distance.

Trustworthiness T and continuity C are those of Venna and Kaski at ten neighbours [[venna2001]] [[venna2006]].
Kruskal is stress-1 on the raw distances at the best map scale.
Sketch-map stress is the mean squared switch residual over all pairs, with the landfold switch, at the best map scale.
Runtimes are asv medians of three runs on one machine with eight cores.
Each timing starts from the descriptor rows and includes any distance matrix the method needs.

Settings are in =landfold/embed.py= of the anneal_repro workflow, with seed 0 throughout.
The versions are scikit-learn 1.9.1, openTSNE 1.0.4, umap-learn 0.5.12 and PHATE 2.0.0.
MDS starts from a random layout with one init, Isomap uses ten neighbours, and t-SNE uses perplexity 30.

Thread use differs between methods.
landfold, openTSNE and PHATE are given all eight cores.
UMAP runs on one thread, because a fixed seed forces it.
The scikit-learn methods keep their defaults, which thread only inside BLAS and OpenMP kernels.
The reference code is serial.
anneal_landfold_serial is landfold on one thread, with a map equal to the parallel one bit for bit.
anneal_landfold_book times the whole path from the book file: reading, DECAF rows and the fold.

The reference sketch-map run takes the landfold landmarks, sigma and switch exponents.
Its landmark weights are Voronoi populations at power 1, as =dimlandmark -w= writes them.
It follows the stages of its own =sketch-map.sh= script.

What the table shows:

- Against the previous paper pipeline, sklearn_mds_switch, landfold raises T by 0.045 to 0.090 and C by 0.005 to 0.080.
  Its sketch-map stress is lower on every book.
  It is 15 to 17 times faster on eight threads and about 2.8 times faster on one.
- Against the reference sketch-map, landfold has higher T on all three books.
  Its C is higher on LJ38 and LJ98 and lower by 0.00003 on LJ75.
  The reference reaches a lower all-pair sketch-map stress, by under 0.001.
  landfold is 128 to 159 times faster on eight threads and 24 to 28 times faster on one.
- t-SNE, openTSNE and UMAP keep more nearest neighbours, with T higher by 0.004 to 0.064.
  Their sketch-map stress is higher on every book, by 0.005 to 0.013, and so is their Kruskal stress.
  The gaps between their clusters carry little of the distance between funnels.
- Raw MDS has the lowest Kruskal stress, since it fits raw distances.
  Its T is 0.04 to 0.09 below landfold.
- Isomap and PHATE are below landfold in T on every book.

Among the methods that keep the global layout, landfold has the highest T on every book.
Only Isomap is as fast, within 0.1 s either way.
