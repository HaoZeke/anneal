* Hessian-vector products

This page explains why a potential handle computes Hessian-vector products
itself and keeps a cached centre.
The functions, their arguments and their errors are in the
[[file:../reference/c_abi.org][C ABI reference]].

A minimum-mode search (Lanczos, dimer) applies the Hessian =H= of the energy to
many directions =v= at one geometry.
With only a force callback each product is a difference of two force
evaluations, and one of them is the centre the search has not moved from.
A potential handle answers =H v= itself and keeps the forces of that centre.

** Cost and error

| Scheme               | Force calls per product        | Error          |
|----------------------+--------------------------------+----------------|
| =RGPOT_HVP_FORWARD=  | 1, plus 1 for each new centre  | =O(h)=         |
| =RGPOT_HVP_CENTRAL=  | 2                              | =O(h^2)=       |
| =RGPOT_HVP_ANALYTIC= | 0                              | the callback's |

The forward scheme reuses the forces of the centre.
=k= forward products at one centre therefore cost =k + 1= force calls instead of
=2k=.
No finite difference makes one call per product at =O(h^2)=.
The forward error is =(h/2) T(u, u) |v|=, with =T= the third derivative of the
energy and =u = v / |v|=, and cancelling it takes the second evaluation that the
central scheme makes.
A search that needs second-order products at no extra force call registers an
analytic product.

=h= is the displacement along the unit direction =u=, in the units of the
positions, and the difference is scaled back by =|v|=.
The forward product is therefore positively homogeneous in =v= but not odd: the
=O(h)= term keeps its sign when =v= changes sign.

** The cached centre

The handle keys its centre on the bits of the positions, the atomic numbers and
the cell.
Comparing bits rather than values keeps the forces computed at =0.0= from being
served at =-0.0=.
Moving one coordinate by one ulp, changing a species or changing the cell is a
new centre.

The key does not see the state behind the callback's =user_data=.
A potential whose parameters change at a fixed geometry calls
=rgpot_potential_hvp_invalidate= to drop the centre.

A search usually needs the centre forces as well as the products.
=rgpot_potential_hvp_centre= returns them from the same cache, so the force call
that the search makes at the centre is the one the forward products reuse.
The handle holds one centre, which matches a search that stays at one geometry
for many products and then moves.

** Analytic products

A potential that knows its Hessian registers the product with
=rgpot_potential_set_hvp_callback=.
=RGPOT_HVP_AUTO= then uses it and makes no force call.
In C++, =rgpot::PotentialHandle::from_impl= registers it for a potential with a
member
=void hessianVectorImpl(const ForceInput &, const double *, double *) const=;
=MorsePot= has one.

** Without the Rust core

=CppCore/rgpot/HessianVector.hpp= gives the same schemes, default step, centre
cache and counters to C++ potentials used without the Rust core.
The Python module uses it.
