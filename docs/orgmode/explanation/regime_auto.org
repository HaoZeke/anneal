#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil author:nil

Policy is *documented, safe, not oracle-optimal* (same stance as
=GaussJacobiQuad= =select_method_auto=).

* Features

| Feature | Source |
|---------+--------|

| =has_grad= | caller supplied gradient |

* Values-only runs under Auto

With ~policy="auto"~, no =grad_fn= and no =noise_sigma=, the driver does
not consult the regime: every such run, whatever its box width or
dimension, goes to one values-only loop over six arms.

| Arm | Starts from |
|-----+-------------|
| =cma= | BIPOP CMA-ES centred on the incumbent: local runs with short steps, large-population runs with a box-wide step; separable above 100 dimensions or when the budget is shorter than the covariance learning horizon; a run stops once its recent best values span less than the success threshold in force |
| =qn= | finite-difference projected BFGS from the incumbent, stepping on a model of the logarithm of its values while they stay positive and are markedly nearer quadratic in it, stopping on the resolution of its values or =1e-7= of the success threshold, kicked from the incumbent once converged |
| =gsa= | one chain from 10 dimensions, and below that one for every eight evaluations of its first slice, between two and four (the first at the incumbent as dual_annealing's =x0=, the others at seeded low-discrepancy points); quenched: each temperature step visits every coordinate once at the cooling visit scale, box-wide early and local late, and keeps only improvements; no local search; once another arm has lowered the incumbent below every chain, the worst chain goes on from there |
| =de= | best/1/bin with the incumbent as the first population member (differential_evolution's =x0=) and the others at seeded low-discrepancy points over the box |
| =surrogate= | an additive model fitted to every evaluation so far: one evaluation tests its per-coordinate minimum, then tempered samples from its marginals |
| =explore= | QMC restarts over the whole box |

Without =x0= the start is the best of a seeded low-discrepancy design of
=dim + 1= points (at most a tenth of the budget), so seeds vary it; with
=x0= it is =x0=. A slice is four gradients' worth of evaluations or a
48th of the budget (eight rounds for each of the six arms), whichever is
larger, but at most the geometric mean of the budget and four gradients'
worth: past 48 times 48 four-gradient slices the number of slices keeps
growing, as the square root of the budget, instead of stopping at 48.
The closing descent's reserve is ten gradients' worth of evaluations,
=10(d+1)=, enough for a descent to converge, but at most 40% of the
budget, and none when that leaves fewer than three gradients' worth.
Turns shorter than eight evaluations are not played.

When the budget affords five finite-difference gradients per
coordinate, =5d(d+1)= evaluations, a descent from the start opens the
run and continues, up to convergence, while each slice lowers the
incumbent by more than the success threshold and by more than =1e-4=
times the largest of the last three gains opening slices have made from
below the start's value, and while its last four slices have gained
more, together, than =1e-4= times the largest such gain. One steep slice
early on then does not end an opening whose descent still pays at the
slower pace of a narrow valley (Rosenbrock on a wide box), while a
descent that creeps along the floor of a side basin, gaining a little
less on every slice, still ends instead of holding the whole run. The
first slice from below the start is held to the gain of the slice from
the start, which ends a descent into a side basin of a multimodal
objective. Those two gains are differences; while every value the run
has found is positive, every later gain, and the gains it is held to,
is a log ratio, =ln(before / after)=, and from the first value at or
below zero every gain is a difference again. A positive loss that
falls by decades from a far start, as a squared error does, then does
not end its opening on the valley floor, held to the gains of the
slices that came down the wall. Until the descent has lowered its own
value from below the start's, a slice in which it stalls short of
convergence, as when its first curvature pair spans a steep wall, or
stops at the start's value, as when every neighbour of a start behind
a sentinel shares it, gives =cma= the slice it takes first short of
the opening budget, once per run, and the opening goes on from the
incumbent =cma= leaves, where the descent restarts. The opening's
successes describe the start, so =qn= then rejoins the ranking on the
prior.

With less, a descent from the start converges only by taking most of
the run, which would leave =gsa= and =de= no turn, and separable
multimodal objectives (shifted Rastrigin, Styblinski-Tang, Levy) need
theirs. Such a run opens instead with one slice of =cma= from the
start. The descent then keeps the turn until a slice, from its second
on, gains less per evaluation than the one before, and =gsa=, whose
quenched visits leave the descent's basin one coordinate at a time,
takes over while each of its slices gains at least as fast as the
descent would have gone on to: the descent's last gain per evaluation
times its ratio to the one before. Until =gsa= has played, the descent
also hands it the turn once fewer than three slices are left beyond the
closing reserve: handing over only the last slice left =gsa= part of
one at twenty to twenty-six evaluations per =d+1=, where the separable
multimodal objectives need its slices. If =gsa='s
first slice falls short, the descent goes on with its own descent, not
restarting at what =gsa= found, and keeps the turn until the run has
used twice the evaluations it had; the hand-over repeats until the
descent stops paying or only the closing reserve is left. With =x0=,
=gsa= therefore gets no turn below about =13.3(d+1) + 15= evaluations
(100 dimensions at 1000 evaluations, 30 at 420) and, just above, one
partial turn that takes what is left beyond the reserve (16 evaluations
at 100 dimensions and 1375, 15 at 30 and 440).

From there =gsa= and then =cma= each keep the turn while they pay. A
phase ends once it has gone eight slices without a success that gains
more than =1e-4= times the phase's largest gain, or as long as the last
such success took if that is longer, so slices that gain crumbs do not
keep it. =gsa='s first phase also ends once its gain per evaluation
since the phase began is no more than that of the descent's last timed
slice; when that slice kicked a converged descent or only confirmed
convergence it gained nothing, and a phase that has gained nothing ends
at its first slice. A =cma= phase that follows it without taking the
turn ends the same way, but only from its eighth slice: on a rotated
objective CMA-ES gains nothing until its step size and covariance have
adapted, while on a smooth valley it would otherwise play slice after
slice that gains a little where the descent had gained faster.

From its fifth slice on, a =gsa= slice that gains less than an eighth
of the phase's best per evaluation lends =cma= a slice, and =cma= takes
the turn if that slice gains faster per evaluation than both the =gsa=
slice and an eighth of the phase's gain per evaluation so far. =gsa=
then lends again only once the run has used twice the evaluations it
had, and never after a lent slice that gained nothing. GSA's records
come in bursts and the slice after one often gains nothing, while a
=cma= slice lent from a record just made gains by refining it: lent
early in a phase, or judged against the one slow slice alone, such a
slice ended phases still finding lower basins (Rastrigin at 10
dimensions). A phase =cma= took likewise lends =gsa= a slice when its
last two slices gain less per evaluation than its lent slice had to
beat, and hands the turn back if =gsa='s slice gains faster than those
two did. The two alternate while a phase hands the other the turn, and
=gsa= resumes after a phase =cma= took from it, for up to eight phases.
If the bar on the first slice from below the start ended the opening
before the descent converged, as from a far start on a curved valley,
and =gsa='s first phase ends at its first slice, the descent takes a
turn before =cma='s phase and keeps it while it pays. After an opening
a phase, and that turn, stop short of the closing reserve plus one
slice for each other arm not yet played, counted again at every slice;
without one, short of the closing reserve plus one slice for =de= until
it has played, since slices held there for the surrogate and the
restart arm cost the phases more than those arms return. Against a
descended incumbent a GSA record is a lower basin and a CMA-ES gain a
better point of the same one; both come in bursts (records as the
anneal cools, a run per covariance) that single slices would not see.

Each arm not yet played then takes a turn, in the table's order (the
warm-up round, as in the main bandit), while the budget holds eight
evaluations beyond the closing reserve, so the last of them may be
short. After it a round picks uniformly with probability =1/round=
(rounds counted from the run's first slice), otherwise replays the arm
whose last turn lowered the incumbent, and otherwise takes a discounted
Thompson draw. A turn is one slice, except that =qn= keeps the turn
while each slice lowers its descent's value by more than the success
threshold, until that descent converges and short of the closing
reserve: a kicked descent climbs before it can beat the incumbent, and
one from a poor start (Lennard-Jones positions with overlapping atoms)
takes many slices to settle, so the turn is scored on the basin the
descent reaches.

So short of the opening budget =cma= plays first, the descent next and
=gsa= when the descent hands it the turn; after an opening =gsa='s
phase plays first and =cma='s next. Where the budget left cannot hold
a phase and the slices it leaves, the warm-up round gives each arm not
yet played a turn in the table's order, as far as the budget goes. =de=
plays in the warm-up round unless the hand-over between the descent and
=gsa= has run into the closing reserve first (Rastrigin at 10
dimensions and 500 evaluations, Lennard-Jones positions at 39 dimensions
and 1000 evaluations), and afterwards mostly when a uniform pick falls
on it. It seldom lowers the incumbent: its population spans the box,
and a turn evolves it a few generations, too few to beat an incumbent
the descent has settled, while on separable objectives =gsa='s
one-coordinate visits make the moves its crossover would, and sooner.

A turn succeeds when it lowers the incumbent by more than the success
threshold in force when it began, =1e-4 max(g, s)=. =g= is the gain of
the last slice that lowered the incumbent from below the start's value.
=s=, the run's resolution, is =eps |f|= of the first value the run
finds below the start's, but never less than =f64::MIN_POSITIVE=, which
it is until then (when the start's value is not finite, every finite
value is below it). The same threshold decides whether the opening goes
on, whether a descent keeps its turn, whether a phase has gained, and
when a CMA-ES run stops: =cma= sets each run's history tolerance to the
threshold in force at every slice it plays. Gains are differences of
values and slices from the start's value record nothing, so a constant
added to the objective changes a threshold only through the resolution
of the shifted values (the rounding of each gain, and =s=), the start's
value sets none, and multiplying the objective by a power of two
multiplies every threshold exactly. The descent likewise stops on the
resolution of its values: an iteration is slow when it lowers its value
by no more than =8 eps= of it, a few units in the last place, or by no
more than =1e-7= of the threshold as it stood when the slice began, so
that near a minimum whose value is small against the gains that set the
threshold the descent stops well before its gains shrink to units in the
last place. Its first trial moves at most 5% of a side whatever the
gradient's size. A first trial accepted with more than six tenths of
the decrease its model predicts gets one longer trial, at the minimiser
of the quadratic through the two values and the slope and up to sixteen
times as long. When the first trial realises all of the predicted
decrease or more, that quadratic has no minimiser, and the longer trial
goes at most halfway from the iterate to the nearest bound along its
line. The descent holds a coordinate at its bound while descending would
carry it out of the box, so a trial carried onto a face can leave the
descent in a minimum of the face, as on
[[*A log-loss fit with minima on its faces][a log-loss fit below]].
Where a face does hold the minimum over the box, holding the longer
trial short of it usually brings the descent there later, so at small
budgets it can end higher, as measured in the same section.

The descent also keeps a second BFGS model, of the logarithm of its
values, while every value it has started from or accepted is positive.
Along a step a quadratic changes by the step times the mean of its
slopes at the two ends, the trapezoid rule, so each curvature pair also
shows how far from quadratic the values and their logarithm are along
its step: the pair counts for the logarithm when the rule misses the
change of the values more than four times as far as the change of their
logarithm, and for the values when it misses the logarithm's that much
further. Once the model of the values has taken two curvature pairs per
coordinate, a pair after which more of the pairs since the models were
last reset count for the logarithm than for the values moves the line
searches onto the model of the logarithm (direction, Armijo test,
extrapolation and interpolation). From then until the models are next
reset the count no longer decides: each step the descent accepts puts
the next line search on the model that predicted the step's value more
closely, the logarithm's on a tie, so a step that the model of the
logarithm predicts less closely moves the line searches back onto the
values, and a later step that it predicts at least as closely moves
them onto the logarithm again. A value at or below zero retires the
model of the logarithm for the rest of the run. A squared error far
from its fit falls through decades along a curved valley, as the mean
squared energy error of a Lennard-Jones parameter fit does, and is far
from quadratic in its values and closer to it in their logarithm. On a
quadratic the rule holds for the values up to the error of the finite
differences, and near a minimum whose value is zero the logarithm is far
from quadratic, so the pairs count for the values; on a loss already on
a logarithmic scale most count for neither. Near such a minimum, as
where a squared error fits exactly, the model of the values can predict
the steps more closely while the count still holds the pairs that came
down the valley, and the steps then move back onto it without waiting
for the count. The test that moves the steps onto the logarithm reads
neither model: on an ill-conditioned quadratic, while the model of the
values is still learning the curvature, the model of the logarithm
predicts many steps more closely without stepping better. Counting
every pair keeps the early pairs of a squared error far from its fit,
and keeps one step along which the logarithm happens to be nearer
quadratic from moving the steps of a descent whose pairs found the
values so. Each pair costs the descent at least one evaluation more
than it has free coordinates, so below =2d(d+1)= evaluations for =d=
free coordinates no line search steps on the logarithm and a run
repeats the loop without that model call for call.
The logarithm enters only through ratios of values, so multiplying the
objective by a power of two still leaves the descent unchanged, while a
constant added to a positive objective changes its logarithm and so can
change its steps.

The surrogate and the restart arm take values in the run's unit, the
power of two at or below the threshold in force when they play: the
surrogate fits values, and sets its temperatures and their floors, in
that unit, and the restart arm's chains weigh each rise in it against
a temperature that starts at one, so they all but never take a rise as
large as the last gain. Multiplying the objective by a power of two
therefore leaves a run unchanged, short of overflow and underflow. The
main bandit's threshold scales with =|f|= instead and floors at 1,
turning absolute below it.

The descent closes the run with the reserve and whatever shorter than
eight evaluations is left before it. It goes on with its current
descent, which may be a kick from the incumbent, and restarts at the
incumbent only if another arm has lowered it since =qn= last played.

** Batch replicas

=anneal.minimize= is the front end for runs from a start point. With one
replica it calls =global_optimize= with =x0=, and the run is the loop
above, call for call. With =replicas=R= it runs =portfolio_replicas=:
=x0= is evaluated once, as a batch of one, and =R= copies of the loop
start from it, each with an even share of the rest of the budget and its
own seed (replica 0 keeps the run's), and each plays the loop as a solo
run of its share and the start would. At each step every replica still
running proposes one point; the points are evaluated as one batch,
through =fun.eval_batch= when there is one, and recorded before any
replica goes on, so a run repeats with its seed.

With the default shared history the replicas keep one archive and so one
incumbent. Arms that start from the incumbent start from the best point
any replica has found, the surrogate fits every replica's evaluations,
and a point another replica has evaluated costs nothing, once per
replica. Replicas that open with a descent from =x0=, or restart one at a
common incumbent, would ask for the same stencil points in the same
order, so replica =r= differences each gradient from coordinate =r d / R=
on instead: the replicas ask for different points of one gradient, which
then takes about =d / R= batches. Replicas that ask for one point in the
same batch share its row, paid for by the one with the most of its share
left, so no replica spends its share on a common descent before the
others, and a step in which they follow one line search is a batch of
fewer than =R= points. With ~history="private"~ every replica keeps its
own archive, as =R= independent runs of a share each batched together
would: the control for what sharing is worth.

* Regimes → preferred portfolio arms / kernels

These rows govern runs with a gradient or a declared noise scale; Legacy
always uses =Default=.

| Regime | When | Prefer |
|--------+------+--------|
| =StochasticNoise= | noise_sigma set | OSA accept required; explore + GSA; refuse exact-only accept path |
| =MultimodalGlobal= | mean box width > 9 and dim ≥ 2 | GSA and DE first, after a short multimodality probe on boxes narrower than 50 |
| =LowDimSmooth= | dim ≤ 5 and gradient | shift/hop/GLE polish-heavy; early QMC polish |
| =HighDimIllConditioned= | dim ≥ 20 and gradient | GLE, reduced-space, hop; surrogates deprioritized |
| =MultimodalNoGrad= | no gradient, dim ≥ 5 or aspect ≥ 8 | reported by =select_regime= but never run by the driver (values-only Auto runs take the loop above) |
| =Default= | else | balanced library order (explore first for restart floor) |

The rows are tested in this order, so a wide box with a gradient is
=MultimodalGlobal= even when dim ≤ 5.

* Safety

- In the main bandit the restart arm (=explore=) always remains first so
  Borel–Cantelli restart still fires. In the values-only loop the floor
  the guarantee needs is asymptotic in the budget. From the opening
  budget on, the opening goes on only while its slices succeed, a phase
  ends once it has gone eight slices, or as long as its last gain took,
  without a success, at most eight phases play, and a descent keeps its
  turn only while each slice
  lowers its value by more than the success threshold. Every success
  lowers the incumbent, and every slice that extends a descent's turn
  lowers that descent's value, by more than =1e-4 s=, and the resolution
  =s= is positive, changes at most once (when the run first finds a
  value below the start's) and never falls below =f64::MIN_POSITIVE=, so
  a bounded objective allows a number of them, and so a length of the
  opening, of each phase and of each descent's turn, that does not grow
  with the budget, while the number of slices does. The =1/round=
  picks, whose sum diverges, then reach every arm. At a given budget
  nothing is reserved for the floor. Short of the opening budget =gsa=
  takes the turn when the descent's gain per evaluation falls, once
  fewer than three slices are left beyond the closing reserve, or after
  a descent that
  converges first, whenever eight evaluations are left beyond that
  reserve. The warm-up round gives =de=, =surrogate= and =explore= a
  slice each when the budget left after the first turns, the phases and
  the descent's turns holds those slices and the closing descent. The
  phases leave room for all of them after an opening and for =de=
  without one; a descent's turn does not. On Lennard-Jones positions at
  39 dimensions and 1000 evaluations =cma= takes the first slice, the
  descent the next and =gsa= the two left short of the closing reserve
  (279 evaluations), the closing descent takes the rest, and =de=,
  =surrogate= and =explore= do not play.
- Unknown features fall to =Default= (safe fallback).
- Policy biases Beta priors and arm *order*, not hard exclusion (except
  noise-aware accept requirement under =StochasticNoise=).
