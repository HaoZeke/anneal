* ASE calculators

=rgpot.ase= is an optional extra (=pip install 'rgpot[ase]'=, ASE 3.23 or
newer).
=import rgpot= does not import ASE.
The in-process calculator evaluates a bound potential in this process.
The Cap'n Proto calculator talks to =potserv=.

ASE itself is described by Hjorth Larsen et al., J. Phys.: Condens.
Matter 29, 273002 (2017), doi:10.1088/1361-648X/aa680e.

** In process

#+begin_src python
from ase.build import bulk
from ase.filters import FrechetCellFilter
from ase.optimize import BFGS
from rgpot.ase import RgpotCalculator

atoms = bulk("Ar", "fcc", a=5.0, cubic=True)
atoms.calc = RgpotCalculator("lj", u0=0.0104, psi=3.40, cutoff=8.5)
BFGS(FrechetCellFilter(atoms), logfile=None).run(fmax=1e-4)
print(atoms.get_potential_energy(), atoms.get_stress())
#+end_src

=RgpotCalculator= is an =ase.calculators.calculator.Calculator=.
Any ASE code that takes a calculator takes it: the optimizers, the cell filters,
molecular dynamics, =ase.mep.NEB=, =ase.vibrations=, and
=ase.calculators.mixing.SumCalculator=.
=tests/python/test_ase_dropin.py= runs each of them.

** Choosing a potential

The first argument is an rgpot potential object or a kind string.
A kind string names any potential class of the build: the class name in any
case, with or without the =Pot= suffix, with =_=, =-=, =.= and spaces ignored.
=lj=, =LJPot=, =morse=, =ljcluster=, =eam_al=, =sw=, =stillingerwebersi=,
=tersoffsic= and =lennard-jones= all resolve.
=rgpot.ase.available_kinds()= lists the strings this build accepts.

The other keyword arguments go to that class's constructor, apart from the
calculator's own =numerical_stress= and =stress_step= and ASE's =label=,
=directory=, =restart=, =ignore_bad_restart_file= and =atoms=.
A name the constructor does not take raises =ValueError= and lists the names it
does take.

#+begin_src python
RgpotCalculator("morse", De=0.3429, a=1.3588, re=2.866, cutoff=7.7)
RgpotCalculator(rgpot.MorsePot(De=0.3429, a=1.3588, re=2.866, cutoff=7.7))
#+end_src

The calculator has no per-element or per-system tables.
It passes the atomic numbers to the kernel unchanged, and the kernel decides
what they mean: LJ and Morse are single-species, the Fortran kernels are
parameterised for their own elements.

** Matching ASE's own calculators

| rgpot                                                             | ASE                                                                   |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =RgpotCalculator("lj", u0=e, psi=s, cutoff=rc)=                   | =LennardJones(epsilon=e, sigma=s, rc=rc, smooth=False)=               |
| =RgpotCalculator("morse", De=e, a=r/r0, re=r0, cutoff=c2*r0, switch_width=(c2-c1)*r0)= | =MorsePotential(epsilon=e, rho0=r, r0=r0, rcut1=c1, rcut2=c2)= |
| =RgpotCalculator("eam_al")=                                       | =EAM= with the closed form of =tests/python/eam_al_reference.py=      |

With those arguments LJ and Morse agree with ASE to better than 1e-13 relative
in energy, forces and stress, and LJ also in per-atom energies and stresses, on
bulk, slab and cluster geometries, including cells narrower than twice the
cutoff.
The tests assert 1e-10.

Both Lennard-Jones forms shift every pair term by its value at the cutoff, so
the force jumps there.
ASE's =smooth=True= multiplies by a cutoff function in \(r^2\) between =ro= and
=rc=; rgpot has no equivalent.
=switch_width= instead multiplies the unshifted pair term by the quintic switch \(S(t) = 1 - 10t^3 + 15t^4 - 6t^5\), \(t = (r - r_\mathrm{on})
/ w\), over \([r_c - w, r_c]\).

ASE's =MorsePotential= is unshifted and always uses its =fcut=, which is the
same quintic in \(1 - t\). rgpot's =MorsePot= with =switch_width= 0 instead
subtracts the pair energy at the cutoff from every pair inside it and does not
taper.
The energies then differ by that shift for each pair and by ASE's taper between
=rcut1 * r0= and =rcut2 * r0=.

ASE ships no EAM-Al table for the kernel's double-exponential aluminium.
ASE's =EAM= accepts callables for the embedding, density and pair functions and
their derivatives, so the reference passes the closed form and no spline.
It agrees with =eam_al= to better than 1e-12 relative in energy and forces.

** Properties and units

Energies are eV, forces are eV/Angstrom, and stress is eV/Angstrom^3 in ASE
Voigt order (xx, yy, zz, yz, xz, xy) with the ASE sign, \(\sigma = V^{-1}
\partial E / \partial \varepsilon\): a compressed crystal has a negative stress.

=implemented_properties= depends on the kernel:

| Property                 | Kernels                                                    |
|--------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| =energy=, =free_energy=, =forces= | every kernel                                      |
| =stress=                 | kernels whose =caps()= report it: LJ, Morse, both Stillinger-Weber classes |
| =energies=               | kernels with =evaluate_per_atom=: LJ, LJ cluster, Morse    |
| =stresses=               | kernels with both: LJ, Morse                               |

=free_energy= equals =energy=: these potentials have no electronic entropy term.
A pair term goes half to each atom in =energies=.
=stresses= is the per-atom virial over the cell volume, the convention of ASE's
=LennardJones=; it sums to =stress=.
Per-atom values are computed only when asked for, so the first
=get_potential_energies()= after =get_potential_energy()= costs one more kernel
call.

=numerical_stress=True= adds =stress= for a kernel that writes none (EAM-Al, EDIP, Lenosky, Tersoff, FeHe, CuH2, WaterH) from a central difference over six cell strains of =stress_step=
(default 1e-6).
That is twelve energy calls a stress.
=get_hessian= raises =NotImplementedError=: no bound potential has an analytic
Hessian.
=ase.vibrations= uses finite differences of the forces and does not need one.

** Periodic boundaries

The kernels are fully periodic and take the minimum image of each pair.
The calculator turns every ASE geometry into a kernel call with the same energy
as the infinite system:

- A periodic cell whose perpendicular width along a periodic axis is at most
  twice the kernel cutoff is tiled into a supercell that is wider.
  The energy is divided by the number of copies and the forces of the first copy
  are returned.
  An 8.5 Angstrom LJ cutoff tiles a cubic 32-atom argon cell, 10.52 Angstrom
  wide, into 8 copies, two along each axis.
- =pbc= false on every axis is a cluster.
  The kernel sees a cube whose side is at least 100 Angstrom and at least the
  cluster extent plus twice the cutoff, so no image enters the cutoff.
  When that side is not longer than twice the extent, it is twice the extent
  plus twice the cutoff instead, so a minimum-image kernel does not fold the
  cluster.
  Energy and forces ignore the caller's cell.
- A mixed =pbc= (a slab or a wire) keeps the periodic cell vectors and replaces
  each non-periodic one with a padded vector orthogonal to them.

Stress is the virial over the caller's cell volume, which is the cell
=calculate_numerical_stress= strains.
A cluster or slab with no volume keeps the padded box's stress.

The cutoff is =config()["cutoff"]= where the binding has one, and otherwise the
kernel's published cutoff.
A kernel with no known cutoff (=ExprPot=, D3, D4, WaterH) is not tiled.
Its cluster box is the 100 Angstrom cube while the cluster spans less than 50
Angstrom, and twice the extent beyond that.
=ljcluster= has free boundaries and raises =ValueError= for a periodic
structure.

** Caching

ASE's =check_state= decides when to call the kernel.
A change of positions, cell, =pbc=, atomic numbers, initial charges or initial
magnetic moments recomputes; anything else returns the stored results.
One kernel call fills energy, forces and stress together, so
=get_potential_energy()=, =get_forces()= and =get_stress()= on unchanged atoms
cost one call.

** Copies, pickling and parameters

=pickle=, =copy.copy= and =copy.deepcopy= rebuild the potential from its class
name and parameters.
The copy has its own kernel instance and the same results.
A calculator over a bound object is rebuilt from that object's =config()=, or
from its no-argument constructor.
A calculator over anything else, or over a bound object that has neither
(=ExprPot=, the engine potentials), raises =TypeError= when pickled; build it
from a kind string with its parameters instead.
=Atoms.copy()= does not copy a calculator, as everywhere in ASE.

=calc.set(u0=...)= rebuilds the potential with the new parameter and resets the
results.
=calc.todict()= holds the class name under =potential= and the parameters.

NEB images may each carry their own calculator, or share one with
=NEB(..., allow_shared_calculator=True)=.

=protocol_reentrant= is True for =__rg_evaluate__= only.
=calculate= stores =atoms= and =results= on the calculator, so two threads need
two calculators.

** Timings

=scripts/bench_ase_calculators.py= times =atoms.get_forces()= under each pair of
calculators above, on fcc crystals displaced by fresh Gaussian noise (0.02
Angstrom) before every call.
Each round times both calculators once in random order; the table is the median
of 21 rounds. rgpot ran with =OMP_NUM_THREADS= unset, which is one thread, on an
8-CPU x86_64 Xeon with Python 3.12 and NumPy 2.5.
The last column is the largest relative energy or force difference between the
pair at one geometry.

The rgpot column includes that cost.

** Batches

=calculate_many= evaluates a list of =Atoms= in one batch.
The default properties are energy and forces.
Pass =attach=True= to store a =SinglePointCalculator= on each structure.
The inputs are not modified.

** RPC calculator

=RgpotRpcCalculator= is the Cap'n Proto calculator.
One connection belongs to one calculator.
A second thread that enters =calculate= while the first still holds the
connection gets =RuntimeError=.
The old import =ase_calculator.RgpotCalculator= warns and re-exports this class.

** Annealing helper

=AnnealRunner= calls =anneal.global_optimize= and writes the best geometry back
onto the atoms.
It is not an =ase.optimize.Optimizer=.
=AnnealOptimizer= is the old name and warns on construction.
