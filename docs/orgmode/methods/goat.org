#+TITLE: GOAT Search Rules

The global optimization algorithm (GOAT) of de Souza runs parallel basin hopping over molecular and atomic clusters.
Its paper is B. de Souza, Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 64, e202500393 (2025).
=anneal_core::goat= carries its search rules, and each rule is a flag on the cluster hop that is off by default.
Each item of the module names the paper behind its rule and its numbers.
Each item also says where anneal departs from that paper.

GOAT states its energies in kcal/mol.
=Config::kcal_per_mol= gives one kcal/mol in the unit of =Config.energy_unit=.
=Config::for_tip4p= declares kilojoules per mole, so there a kcal/mol is 4.184 energy units.
A run in reduced units has no chemical unit, so there a kcal/mol is read as one =Config.energy_scale=.

Anneal's own method is communicating chains with the superbasin tracker on.
The example binaries run it with =comm,sbasin= and =HISTORY_REPLICAS= under =HISTORY=shared=.
Each rule below says whether it applies on top of that baseline.

* Ladder and self-regulating temperature

=Config::with_goat_ladder= sets four rungs that never exchange states.
Rung =k= starts at the temperature where an uphill step of =LADDER_STEPS_KCAL[k]= kcal/mol is accepted half the time.
GOAT's acceptance is =exp(-dE / k_B T)=, so that temperature is =step / ln 2=.
The steps are GOAT's 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 kcal/mol, so the ladder doubles from rung to rung.
The ladder turns minima hopping off, since GOAT's walk is Metropolis.

=Config.self_regulating_temperature= moves a chain's temperature by =TEMPERATURE_FACTOR= after each outcome.
An accepted hop to another basin divides the temperature by the factor.
A refused one multiplies it by the factor, and the temperature never falls below the base.
The factor of 1.05 is GOAT's =T_fac=.
The feedback settles at one acceptance in two for any factor, and the factor sets only its speed.
Minima hopping has no Metropolis test, so the flag does nothing there.

The ladder cannot run on the communicating baseline.
=EnsembleConfig::validate= refuses a chain with more than one rung, because a ladder owns its own bias.
The binaries therefore run it as a separate single-process arm with =goat=.
They stop when =goat= meets =HISTORY_REPLICAS=.
That arm is one process with its own bias and Metropolis acceptance, so it compares two methods and not one rule.
=selft= applies on each chain of the baseline when the chain is Metropolis.
That is the Lennard-Jones preset and not the TIP4P one, whose chains use minima hopping.

#+begin_src bash
SEED_OFFSET=100 HISTORY_REPLICAS=4 HISTORY=shared HISTORY_POLICY=accepted \
cargo run --release --features ira,bank-rpc --example lj_cluster_search -- \
  38 400000 1 comm,sbasin,selft
SEED_OFFSET=100 \
cargo run --release --features ira,bank-rpc --example lj_cluster_search -- \
  38 400000 1 comm,sbasin,plainsurface,goat
#+end_src

* Boost after a stall

=Config.boost_on_stall= accepts a chain's next new minimum after a stall, whatever its energy.
GOAT's gradient boost takes that step after =GradBoost= iterations without a new minimum, three by default.
A new minimum is a quenched trial whose descriptor the chain's own bias has never deposited on.
The stall length here is not GOAT's count of three, which counts GOAT's own uphill walks.
A chain is stalled once its run of trials without a new minimum reaches a length set by its own discovery rate.
That length is the longest run the rate shows about once.
=goat::expected_longest_run= gives it from the leading term of Schilling's longest-run law.
Schilling's law is in College Math. J. 21, 196 (1990).
The hop has no gradient reflection, so only the forced acceptance carries over.

The boost applies on every chain of the baseline, under the Metropolis rule and under minima hopping.
Its stall counter and its test for a new minimum read the chain's own bias, not the shared history.
=boost= turns it on in both binaries, and each chain then prints its count of boosts on its =goat:= line.

* Rotamer test

=Config.rotamer_test= counts two minima as one basin when GOAT's sorting calls them the same structure.
GOAT takes the criteria from the Conformer-Rotamer Ensemble Sampling Tool (CREST).
CREST is the tool of Pracht, Bohle and Grimme, PCCP 22, 7169 (2020).
Two minima match when their energies and their rotational constants agree.
The energy window is =ROTAMER_WINDOW_KCAL=, CREST's 0.05 kcal/mol, which is narrower than GOAT's 0.1 kcal/mol.
The rotational constants agree when their largest relative difference is below a tolerance.
The tolerance is GOAT's 1 percent for a spherical top.
It widens to 2.5 percent, the top of CREST's range, for an anisotropic rotor.
CREST gives that range in Pracht et al., J. Chem. Phys. 160, 114110 (2024), and not the curve between its ends.
The curve here is anneal's, a straight line in the anisotropy =1 - I_min / I_max=.
The relative difference of two rotational constants equals that of their moments of inertia.
The test therefore reads the principal moments.
GOAT's second test, an aligned distance below 0.125 angstrom, is not carried.
The descriptor merge of the hop already applies in its place.

A minimum the descriptor merge does not place then takes the descriptor of a kept rotamer.
The bias key of a screened trial that was never fully quenched goes through the test as well.
=Outcome.rotamers= leaves such trials out of its count.
Masses are standard atomic weights of the rigid-body sites or of =Config.species=.
A Lennard-Jones run names no species, so its points carry unit masses.

The test acts on one chain's own store.
That store is the chain's bias key and, under minima hopping, its controller's basin identity.
The shared history of an ensemble keeps its exact witness and does not consult the rotamer archive.
The rotamer test is a tolerance test that can join two distinct minima, and the history is the record of exact identity.
The superbasin tracker keeps the descriptor merge as well.
=rotamer= turns the test on in both binaries, and each chain then prints its count of merges on its =goat:= line.
