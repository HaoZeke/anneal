* The molecular local step

A cluster search relaxes every proposal before it judges it.
By default that relaxation is warm-started L-BFGS.
=LocalStep::Anc= offers a second relaxation for molecules, the approximate normal coordinate (ANC) scheme of xtb's ANCopt.
This page explains what it does, how it differs from xtb, and what it was measured to do.
[[file:../howto/anc-local-step.org][Use the ANC local step]] says how to turn it on.

** Why a model Hessian

A quasi-Newton relaxation in Cartesian coordinates starts from a unit Hessian.
It then has to learn, one curvature pair at a time, that a bond stretch is a hundred times stiffer than a torsion.
On a flexible molecule that learning is most of the relaxation.
Molecular optimisers that converge in tens of cycles start instead from a model Hessian built from the geometry alone, and step in the coordinates that diagonalise it.

** What it does

=methods::anc_opt::AncOpt= follows xtb 6.7.1 (=optimizer.f90=, =type/anc.f90=, =bfgs.f90=, =model_hessian.f90=):

1. Build a model Hessian at the current geometry.
   The default is Lindh's 1995 valence model, with xtb's prefactors 0.40, 0.13 and 0.0075 for stretch, bend and torsion.
   Swart and Bickelhaupt's covalent-radius weight is the alternative.
2. Add xtb's damped C6 term to the stretch of every pair, with D2 coefficients scaled by 20.
   As in xtb's =ddvopt=, the Cartesian Hessian of the attraction \(V = -s_6 C_6 f(r)/r^6\) is subtracted from the pair block.
   Beyond the damping radius that gives the pair a positive radial curvature, so a hydrogen-bonded or dispersion-bound complex does not look like separate molecules to the model.
3. Project out rigid translations and rotations, diagonalise, shift the spectrum so the softest mode sits at =hlow=, and clamp it to =[hlow, hmax]=.
   The eigenvectors are the coordinates.
4. Take rational function steps in those coordinates, each component capped at =max_component= bohr.
   A step \(q\) maps back to Cartesians linearly, \(x = x_\mathrm{ref} + Bq\), where \(B\) holds the eigenvectors and \(x_\mathrm{ref}\) is the geometry they were built at.
5. Update the coordinate Hessian after each step with BFGS (with xtb's diagonal floor) or Bofill's update.
6. Rebuild the coordinates from a fresh model Hessian every =micro_cycles= steps.
   The rebuild resets the coordinate Hessian to the new model eigenvalues.
   With the trust radius off, a proposed step longer than 2 bohr after the second in a basis also forces a rebuild.

** Where it departs from xtb

- The convergence test is the caller's: the largest Cartesian gradient component under =gtol=, the same test =WarmLbfgs= applies.
  The two optimisers therefore count calls to the same gate.
- =HessianUpdate::Bofill= is Bofill's update.
  xtb's alternative to BFGS is Powell's.
- =AncOpt::trust_radius= is off by default, as xtb has none.
  When set, a step that raises the energy by more than =energy_rise= is rejected and the radius shrinks.
  The coordinate Hessian is updated only with kept steps.
  When the model predicted no descent, the ratio test does not grow the radius.
  A step longer than the radius is scaled to it rather than forcing a rebuild, and xtb's 1.5 lengthening of steps at a small gradient is off.

** How it plugs into a search

=Config::local_step= selects the relaxation.
The search uses ANC only when the configuration names =species= and the state has \(3N\) coordinates; on any other state it falls back to L-BFGS.
Every value-and-gradient call is charged to the search's ledger, exactly as L-BFGS calls are.
A call that returns a non-finite value or gradient is counted as refused, and a refused first call refuses the start, the same accounting L-BFGS gets.
=Config::local_step= is left out of the resolved configuration while it is L-BFGS, so the hashes of existing configurations do not change.

** Measured

The engine was GFN2-xTB through the =xtb= 6.7.1 executable, one process and one counted call per value and gradient, with =OMP_NUM_THREADS=1= and =MKL_CBWR=COMPATIBLE=.
The starts were RDKit ETKDG geometries with no force-field cleanup, four seeds each of met-enkephalin (75 atoms), trofinetide (43 atoms) and N-methylacetamide with two waters (18 atoms).
Both optimisers ran from each start to a largest gradient component of 5e-3 eV/Å, with =examples/local_opt_bench.rs=.
[[file:../howto/anc-local-step.org][Use the ANC local step]] gives the command for one row.
Each end point was then given to =xtb --hess=.
The whole set was run twice, and the two runs print the same lines and write the same final geometries.

The official =xtb= binary links Intel MKL, and =MKL_CBWR= pins the code path MKL would otherwise pick from the processor.
L-BFGS is sensitive to that path.
On one machine, metenk-3 under L-BFGS took between 1274 and 1363 calls across MKL's code paths, and every run ended within 0.3 meV of the others.
On metenk-2, the unpinned path stopped in a minimum 115 meV higher, after 1294 calls.

The rule for "same stationary point" was written before the run: an energy difference of at most 1 meV and a heavy-atom Kabsch RMSD of at most 0.1 Å.
Imaginary modes smaller than 20 cm⁻¹ are noted but not counted.

Every run met the gate, and =xtb --hess= finds no imaginary mode at any of the 24 end points, so all are minima.

ANC took between 2.8 and 7.5 times fewer calls on every start.
On four starts it reached the same minimum as L-BFGS: nma2w-0, -1 and -2, and trofinetide-2.
On the other eight it reached a different one: all four met-enkephalin starts, nma2w-3, and trofinetide-0, -1 and -3.
Two of those miss the rule narrowly, nma2w-3 on energy (1.7 meV) and trofinetide-3 on RMSD (0.107 Å).
ANC ended higher on six of the eight, by up to 406.5 meV on metenk-3, and lower on metenk-1 and trofinetide-3.
With the trust radius on (=anc-trust= in the bench, a starting radius of 0.3 bohr), ANC reaches a different minimum on eight of the twelve starts and ends higher on six of them.
All its end points are minima; one, trofinetide-0, has a −5.1 cm⁻¹ mode below the 20 cm⁻¹ threshold.

** Why L-BFGS stays the default

A local step inside a search is judged by where it lands, not only by how fast.
The different minima come from the ANC step itself.
Each step is a rational function step in the eigenvectors of the model Hessian, mapped back to Cartesians by the linear update \(x = x_\mathrm{ref} + Bq\).
That frame is held for =micro_cycles= steps and then rebuilt, and the rebuild resets the coordinate Hessian to the model eigenvalues.
On a peptide the frame carries the relaxation out of the torsional valley that L-BFGS stays in.

metenk-3 shows the size of the effect.
ANC ends 406.5 meV above L-BFGS, with a heavy-atom RMSD of 2.409 Å between the end points.
Both runs stop on the same test, the largest Cartesian gradient component, at 3.94e-3 eV/Å for L-BFGS and 4.43e-3 eV/Å for ANC, and both end points are minima.
The trust radius rejects a step that raises the energy by more than =energy_rise=, scales a long step to the radius, and drops the 1.5 lengthening.
With it on, metenk-3 still ends 406.9 meV above L-BFGS, at a heavy-atom RMSD of 2.403 Å.
Shorter, guarded steps leave the gap in place.

Until a search-level measurement shows that the saved calls buy more hops than the changed minima cost, ANC stays opt-in.
