#+TITLE: Method Catalog

Every algorithmic mechanism in =anneal-core=, grouped by role. Each entry
gives what the mechanism does, its key types and functions with
=file:line= references, the constants or defaults it fixes, and what it
feeds or consumes elsewhere in the crate. Read alongside
[[file:composition.org][composition.org]], which walks one cooperative run end
to end and names each mechanism as it fires.

* Local search

Mechanisms that operate inside one replica's own perturb-quench chain:
deciding whether a trial is worth finishing, which move to try, when the
chain is trapped, and how to leave.

** Screening quench predictor

What it is: a running fit to a partial relaxation's energy trace that
decides, before the relaxation finishes, whether it can possibly beat the
incumbent. It extrapolates the geometric decay of successive energy
decrements in log space and reports a verdict once the extrapolated limit
is separated from the incumbent by a confidence margin.

Key types and functions: =Prediction=, =Verdict= (=Hopeless= /
=Promising= / =Undecided=), =QuenchPredictor= at
src/quench.rs:96, its =predict= method (src/quench.rs:159) and =observe=
(src/quench.rs:133).

Constants and defaults: =warmup = 4= decrements before any verdict is
allowed (three decrements is the minimum to fit a rate and a residual);
=confidence = 2.0= standard deviations; =margin = 1e-3= extra separation
beyond the fitted uncertainty (src/quench.rs:115-123).

Feeds/consumes: wired into the driver's =adaptive_screen= config flag
(src/methods/cluster_hopping/config.rs:554) and the staged/screened
relaxation logic inside =run_with_gradient= in
src/methods/cluster_hopping.rs. Complementary to, and separately switched
from, the Bayesian screen below.

** Bayesian cost-asymmetric screen

What it is: a conjugate Bayesian linear model (Normal-Inverse-Gamma
posterior) over the drop between a cheap partial ("screened") relaxation
and the full one, used to decide whether a trial deserves the full,
charged relaxation. The decision threshold is derived from the relative
cost of a wasted full relaxation against a missed improvement, not tuned
by hand.

Key types and functions: =Screen= (src/screen.rs:62) with =observe=,
=predict=, =decide=, =probability_of_improvement=;
=cost_asymmetric_threshold= (src/screen.rs:46); =DropModel=
(src/screen.rs:221), the companion model of the remaining energy drop a
purchased screen still owes.

Constants and defaults: threshold derivation
=p > Q / (Q + R) = (R - S) / (2R - S)= where =S= is screen steps and =R=
is full relax steps; for the measured hop (=S=25=, =R=200=) this is
=7/15= (src/screen.rs:38-45). Ridge prior precision =1e-3 * I=,
inverse-gamma =a0 = b0 = 1e-3= (src/screen.rs:97-99). Config-side knobs:
=bayes_screen=, =bayes_exploration=, =bayes_threshold=, =bayes_warmup=
(src/methods/cluster_hopping/config.rs:275, 377-383).
=Config::derived= sets =bayes_threshold= from
=cost_asymmetric_threshold(screen_steps, relax_steps)=
(src/methods/cluster_hopping/config.rs:710-717).

Feeds/consumes: gates whether a trial proceeds to full relaxation inside
the hop loop; independent of, and switchable alongside, the quench
predictor and =return_screen=.

** Return screen

What it is: abandons a trial whose short relaxation is heading straight
back into the current basin, before paying for the full relaxation. Near
a deep minimum roughly nineteen proposals in twenty return, so most of
the budget otherwise buys relaxations into the basin already occupied.

Key types and functions: config flags =return_screen=, =return_polish=,
=return_polish_after= (src/methods/cluster_hopping/config.rs:459-479).

Constants and defaults: measured separation on a shape distance after a
partial relaxation, returns vs. escapes: 0.160 vs. 1.846, 97% of pairs
ordered correctly at thirty iterations (config.rs:452-458). On by
default in =Config::recommended= (src/methods/cluster_hopping/config.rs:674).

Feeds/consumes: complements the Bayesian screen; both exist to spend
charged evaluations only where they are likely to pay.

** Staged quench

What it is: before the full screening relaxation, settles only the
moved atoms (one to three, for the measured-productive moves) in their
frozen environment. A k-atom settle costs a fraction of the full
=n(n-1)/2= pair evaluation, charged fractionally through
=Ledger::charge_frac= (src/methods/cluster_hopping.rs:289).

Key types and functions: config flags =staged_quench=, =settle_iters=
(src/methods/cluster_hopping/config.rs:349-351).

Feeds/consumes: cheapens the first stage the screening quench predictor
and Bayesian screen both act on.

** Move kernels (classical typed-algebra layer)

What it is: the crate's generic simulated-annealing =MoveKernel= trait
and its concrete proposal distributions, shared by the classical SA
drivers (portfolio, parallel tempering) rather than specific to cluster
search.

Key types and functions: =MoveKernel<T>= trait (src/movekernel.rs:16);
=Gaussian= (src/movekernel.rs:32), isotropic normal proposal;
=Cauchy= (src/movekernel.rs:64), heavy-tailed symmetric proposal, the
=q_v = 2= case of the generalized visiting distribution; =TsallisVisit=
(src/movekernel.rs:137), the Tsallis/GSA visiting kernel with tail index
=q_v in (1,3)=, sampled by the Schuur/Xiang transform used by GenSA and
SciPy =dual_annealing=.

Constants and defaults: =VISIT_TAIL_LIMIT = 1.0e8= (src/movekernel.rs:143),
matching SciPy's tail clip. Literature default =q_v = 2.7=.

Feeds/consumes: implements law L2 of the typed algebra (proposal support
must lie within the neighborhood); used directly by
=ClusterMove::Visit= (below) and by the portfolio's Tsallis-visit arm.

** Cluster proposal library

What it is: the cluster-specific move set and the enum that dispatches
among its members. A =MoveLibrary= is one named combination of kernels
selected by a preset; =ClusterMove= is the concrete kernel enum, kept
as an enum rather than a trait object so the generic =propose= stays
monomorphized per call.

Key types and functions: =MoveLibrary= (src/methods/cluster_hopping/moves.rs:12)
with variants =Atomic=, =Lean=, =LeanBurst=, =Visit=, =Twin=,
=SelfReseed=, =LearnedReseed=, =Reseed=, =GrowthAndTwin=, =Molecular=;
its =kernels= method (moves.rs:50) composes the concrete arms. =ClusterMove=
enum (moves.rs:176) with variants =AllPoints=, =SinglePoint=, =Visit=,
=Burst= (composed surface relocations, one acceptance test per excursion),
=GroupRelocate=/=GroupBurst=/=GroupShake= (rigid molecular analogues),
=SurfaceRelocate=, =ShellRotate=, =Symmetrise=, =Twin= (reflect across a
dense plane), =Reseed= (regrow a local order from a template, the only
kernel that crosses a funnel boundary in one step; see
[[file:catalog.org::*Descriptors and identity][descriptors]]/=crate::lattice=), =Angular= (Wales-Doye worst-bound-point
move), =Soap= (descriptor-space pullback step; see the SOAP entry below).
=worst_bound=/=worst_bound_scaled= (moves.rs:434/445) decide when a point
is loose enough for the angular move. =connectivity_groups=
(moves.rs:602) derives rigid molecular groups from the current bonding
rather than a fixed declaration.

Constants and defaults: =LennardJonesPreset= and =MolecularPreset=
(src/methods/cluster_hopping/preset.rs) fix the dimensionless
coefficients (e.g. =MERGE_RADIUS = 0.7=, =TEMPERATURE = 0.8=,
=BIAS_HEIGHT = 0.25=, =SOAP_RMSD = 0.35=, =SOAP_CUTOFF = 3.5=,
=MIN_SEPARATION = 0.85=) multiplied by declared length/energy scales.
=Config::recommended= (config.rs:661) selects =MoveLibrary::LeanBurst=
with the SOAP arm and Thompson allocation over the arms.

Feeds/consumes: selected per-config by =MoveLibrary::kernels=; chosen
per-hop by the allocators below (=allocate_moves= / =contextual_moves= /
=depth_reward=); the =Soap= variant consumes the descriptor-space
pullback machinery.

** Basin-identity and collective-variable bias

What it is: two complementary bias mechanisms. =WellTemperedBias=
deposits a Gaussian bump on a hand-chosen low-dimensional collective
variable (Barducci-Bussi-Parrinello well-tempered metadynamics).
=BasinBias= instead keys deposits on discrete basin identity via a
=Fingerprint=, removing the need to choose a CV axis; it is the superbasin
escape acceleration of Chatterjee and Voter with the well-tempered weight
on top. =BasinIndex= splits out the identity-only half (no potential
attached) for mechanisms that need "have I been here before" without
depositing.

Key types and functions: =Bias= trait (src/bias.rs:18); =WellTemperedBias=
(src/bias.rs:46); =BasinBias<F: Fingerprint>= (src/bias.rs:292);
=BasinIndex<F: Fingerprint>= (src/bias.rs:308); =Fingerprint= trait
(src/bias.rs:522); =SortedPairs= (src/bias.rs:533), the default
permutation/translation/rotation-invariant fingerprint for point sets.

Constants and defaults: deposit rule
=w_k = w_0 * exp(-V_{k-1}(s_k) / ((gamma - 1) * T))=; config knobs
=bias_height=, =bias_gamma=, =merge_radius=, =adaptive_height=,
=height_revisits= (config.rs:75-94, 508-510). Measured motivation: at 38
points the ico/decahedral fourth Steinhardt parameter separates by 0.19,
at 75 points by only 0.023 (narrower than a sensible deposition width),
which motivates keying on identity instead (src/bias.rs:271-291).

Feeds/consumes: consumed by the hop loop's acceptance/tabu logic in
=run_with_gradient=; the fingerprint can be =SortedPairs= (descriptor
space) or, with the =ira= feature, =IraPassthrough=/=IraMetric= from
[[file:catalog.org::*IRA/SOFI canonical order and shape distance][the shape module]].

** Density-of-states, flat histogram, and energy bias

What it is: three related mechanisms that target the *quenched-energy*
axis instead of (or on top of) coordinate/descriptor space, because a
per-basin bias cannot fill a whole funnel of exponentially many basins.
=DensityOfStates= builds a Wang-Landau-style posterior over =S = ln g=
on a binned energy axis. =CutWeight= is a flat-below/suppressed-above
target (flat sampling over the whole range wastes budget on the
high-energy sea). =EnergyBias= deposits a well-tempered bias directly in
quenched energy, filling the funnel floor rather than one basin at a
time.

Key types and functions: =DensityOfStates= (src/dos.rs:96); =Weight=
(src/dos.rs:539); =CutWeight= (src/dos.rs:617) and =cut_from=
(src/dos.rs:640); =EnergyBias= (src/dos.rs:972) and
=EnergyBias::from_sample= (src/dos.rs:1006).

Constants and defaults: =BINS = 96= (src/dos.rs:92);
=EnergyBias::FILL_DEPOSITS = 100.0=, the number of deposits that fill a
one-standard-deviation well (src/dos.rs:993); tempering set by
=(gamma - 1) T = sigma= and =w0 = sigma / FILL_DEPOSITS=, so no scale is
system-specific. Config knobs: =flat_histogram=, =flat_sweep=,
=flat_quantile=, =statistical_temperature=, =energy_bias=
(config.rs:284-306). Measured: at 38 points, flat-over-visited-range
solved 3/24 seeds vs. 15/24 for plain Metropolis (src/dos.rs:594-616);
per-basin bias alone left 12 of 17 failing seeds stuck at exactly the
same icosahedral-funnel floor energy (src/dos.rs:947-956).

Feeds/consumes: an alternative/complementary acceptance and deposit
target to =BasinBias=, wired into the same hop loop
(src/methods/cluster_hopping.rs:1958-2050 region shows the deposit and
sweep-refresh logic inline).

** Stall escapes

What it is: a family of mechanisms triggered only when the chain has
stopped improving, each answering a different way of being stuck.

- Soft-mode climb (=escape_on_stall=, config.rs:156): Lanczos
  soft-mode estimate, then climbs past the saddle with feedback
  overshoot. Escalates over =minima_hopping='s always-on scaled move.
  Costs ~637 charged evaluations vs. ~30 for a plain hop; run only on
  stall it costs a few percent of budget (config.rs:147-155).
- Trail-on-stall (=trail_on_stall=, config.rs:165): re-enters the
  basin's *entry point* (the accepted trial partway down from the
  ridge) with an outward push, cheaper than re-climbing.
- Tabu quarantine (=tabu_on_stall=, config.rs:403): outright rejects
  states within the merge radius of a quarantined structure,
  replacing a soft well-tempered penalty that measurably failed to
  outrun the entropic lockout (config.rs:384-402).
- Restart-on-stall (=restart_on_stall=, config.rs:420): restarts the
  walker from a fresh random configuration but *keeps the bias*, so
  the landscape memory (not the walker) survives.
- Path-on-stall (=path_on_stall=, config.rs:487; src/path.rs):
  =transverse_path= (src/path.rs:110) relaxes images perpendicular to
  the path tangent between the current structure and an archive
  member, so the corridor between funnels is sampled instead of
  jumped; =interpolate_path= (src/path.rs:188) relaxes interpolated
  images; =StallDetector= (src/path.rs:246) decides when hopping has
  stalled enough to justify the expense.
- Symmetrise-on-stall (=symmetrise_on_stall=, config.rs:219;
  src/symmetrise.rs): =detect= (src/symmetrise.rs:202) finds the best
  approximate rotational symmetry over axes derived from the inertia
  tensor and centroid-to-point/pair-midpoint directions;
  =symmetrise= (src/symmetrise.rs:285) averages each point around its
  orbit; withheld when the structure is already icosahedrally
  dominated (=ih_dominated=, src/soap.rs:1088), so the mechanism never
  invents a packing the observed cloud does not have.
- Funnel tracking (=track_funnels=, config.rs:204; src/funnel_spectral.rs):
  builds a transition graph on accepted hops keyed by basin identity;
  =Partition= (src/funnel_spectral.rs:43) and =FunnelSpectrum=
  (src/funnel_spectral.rs:85) compute the Fiedler vector of the
  normalised Laplacian as a continuous collective variable for an
  extra well-tempered bias, replacing a hand-chosen order parameter.

Constants and defaults: escape trigger is
=escape_stall_factor= times the longest quiet stretch already survived,
not a fixed patience (config.rs:166-182, measured: fixed patience 400
scored 2/8 seeds vs. 9/16 without it). =STALL_LEAVE=-style thresholds
recur elsewhere in the crate at value 8 (see
[[file:catalog.org::*Catalog policy][catalog policy]] and [[file:catalog.org::*Cooperative wells and swarm role][swarm role]]).

Feeds/consumes: all gated behind the same stall detector state inside
=run_with_gradient=; several (path, symmetrise, tabu) read the bias's
or the archive's own history rather than any external oracle.

** Calibrated merge radius and diversity annealing

What it is: rather than a hand-set merge radius (which does not
transfer between cluster sizes or descriptor metrics), =calibrate_radius=
sets the radius from the step length an accepted hop actually reaches.
=DiversityAnnealer= separately anneals a *population* diversity
threshold from wide to narrow across the budget, for bank-style runs.
=StepCalibrator= is the general online-quantile primitive both build on.

Key types and functions: =StepCalibrator= (src/calibrate.rs:34);
=DiversityAnnealer= (src/diversity.rs:58) and
=DiversityAnnealer::from_population=.

Constants and defaults: config knobs =calibrate_radius=,
=calibrate_quantile=, =calibrate_warmup=, =diversity_floor=
(config.rs:428-432, 499-507). Measured: annealing 0.7 down to 0.07 took
a run from 250 basins at 25 revisits to 4423 basins at 2.6 revisits, and
the best found from -396.282 to -394.629 (config.rs:503-506).

Feeds/consumes: sets =merge_radius= consumed by =BasinBias= and by the
[[file:catalog.org::*Population methods][CSA bank]]'s admission threshold.

** Escape activation (softest-mode climb)

What it is: climbs a structure out of its current basin along the
softest curvature direction rather than displacing it randomly.
Barkema and Mousseau (Phys. Rev. Lett. 77, 4358, 1996) and Malek and
Mousseau (Phys. Rev. E 62, 7723, 2000): push along the mode, relax the
perpendicular components to stay on the valley floor, and repeat until
the curvature along the mode turns negative, which means the ridge is
behind and a quench from there falls into a different basin. Measured
on LJ38: 576 of 959 straight-displacement quenches returned to the
basin they left and only 10 found anything new, against activation's
crossing test (activation.rs:9-11).

Governing stopping rule: climb while \(\lambda_{\min} \geq 0\) or
\(\mathrm{sign}\cdot(g\cdot v) \geq 0\); stop and overshoot once
\(\lambda_{\min} < 0\) and \(\mathrm{sign}\cdot(g\cdot v) < 0\)
(activation.rs:206-221), where \(\lambda_{\min}\) is the softest
curvature from a Lanczos pass and \(v\) is the direction of travel
along the mode.

Key types and functions: =Activation= config (src/methods/activation.rs:37);
=ActivationOutcome= (activation.rs:93); =activate= (activation.rs:114,
the climb loop: curvature pass via =crate::curvature::curvature_features=,
perpendicular relaxation capped by =perp_max_move=, refresh cadence
=refresh=, overshoot past the turning point).

Constants and defaults: =step = 0.2=, =max_steps = 24=, =perp_steps = 3=,
=perp_rate = 0.02=, =perp_max_move = 0.05=, =lanczos_steps = 12=,
=refresh = 3=, =overshoot = 1.5= (activation.rs:77-88); the
=perp_max_move= cap exists because a fixed rate is unsafe on a
gradient spanning decades -- measured on LJ38, an uncapped
perpendicular step reached a minimum in only 6 of 1589 relaxations,
returning a structure with gradient 1.0 where a minimum has 1e-6
(activation.rs:52-58).

Feeds/consumes: consumes =crate::curvature::curvature_features= for
the Lanczos mode and shares the perpendicular-projection convention
with =crate::path= (activation.rs:27-30); called from
=cluster_hopping='s soft-mode-climb stall response
(cluster_hopping.rs:40, 2475-2478), documented as a black-box call
site under [[file:catalog.org::*Stall escapes][stall escapes]] above
("Soft-mode climb ... Escalates over =minima_hopping='s always-on
scaled move"); this section documents the climb mechanism itself.
=EscapeFeedback= (below) sets how far to climb; =activate= decides
when to stop.

** Minima-hopping escape feedback (=EscapeFeedback=)

What it is: history-conditioned escape feedback after Goedecker's
minima hopping (Goedecker, J. Chem. Phys. 120, 9911, 2004), distinct
from and complementary to this crate's basin bias. Basin bias raises
the *potential* on a revisited basin (Metropolis on \(F+V\)); escape
feedback instead scales the *next escape attempt*, growing
geometrically on a revisit and shrinking on a discovery, so a chain
that keeps returning to one basin escalates until it leaves while a
chain finding new structures settles down to explore them. Goedecker
argues against flooding transition regions for the same reason this
crate keeps the two mechanisms separate: the corridor between funnels
must stay crossable, so the response to a revisit should be a harder
push, not a higher potential where the chain needs to pass
(minima_hopping.rs:20-29). Schoenborn, Goedecker, Roy and Oganov
(J. Chem. Phys. 130, 144108, 2009) add feedback proportional to visit
count and report this finds the LJ75 Marks decahedron where a
cut-and-splice evolutionary algorithm does not.

Governing update: on a revisit, escape scale
\(s \mathrel{*}= \beta_{same}\) (same basin) or
\(s \mathrel{*}= \beta_{known}(1+c\,v)\) (a different basin seen \(v\)
times before, the Schoenborn enhancement); on a discovery,
\(s \mathrel{*}= \beta_{new} = 1/\beta_{same}\); \(s\) is clamped to
its floor/ceiling on every update (=observe=,
src/methods/minima_hopping.rs:162, defaults
\(\beta_{same}=\beta_{known}=1.05\), \(c=0.1\)). Acceptance of an
energy rise \(\delta\): accept iff \(\delta < \tau\), then
\(\tau \mathrel{*}= \alpha_{accept}=1/1.05\) on acceptance or
\(\tau \mathrel{*}= \alpha_{reject}=1.05\) on rejection, clamped to
\([10^{-9}, 10^6]\) (=accept=, minima_hopping.rs:192) -- an adaptive
threshold rather than a Metropolis temperature, since "a temperature
cold enough to polish cannot cross and one hot enough to cross cannot
polish" (minima_hopping.rs:188-191).

Key types and functions: =Visit= (minima_hopping.rs:42, =Same=/
=Known=/=New=); =EscapeFeedback= (minima_hopping.rs:58); =observe=
(minima_hopping.rs:162); =accept= (minima_hopping.rs:192);
=discovery_rate= (minima_hopping.rs:210).

Constants and defaults: =escape_ceiling = 4x= the starting scale
(minima_hopping.rs:89), priced by measurement: at a ceiling of 64 an
LJ38 run paid 138 charged evaluations per hop against 39 without the
controller, buying 2173 hops instead of 7775 at the same budget and
solving 1 seed in 8 where the plain chain solved 8 -- "escalation that
prices itself out of the budget is not escalation" (minima_hopping.rs:84-88).

Feeds/consumes: constructed and driven by
=cluster_hopping::run_with_gradient= (=EscapeFeedback::new= at
cluster_hopping.rs:1032, =feedback.observe= at
1169/1861/1869/2497, =feedback.escape()= scaling the library move at
1344-1348 and 2465, =feedback.accept= gating uphill trials at 1870),
documented as a black-box call site under
[[file:catalog.org::*Stall escapes][stall escapes]] above; this
section documents the feedback mechanism itself.

** Objective-driven cluster search (=search=)

What it is: the caller-facing entry point that wraps
=cluster_hopping::optimize_with_gradient= around any
=DifferentiableObjective<f64>=, building the relaxation, charging every
evaluation to the ledger, and counting convergence outcomes. Exists so
callers stop hand-writing the same relax/charge/count glue: an rgpot
potential reaches the driver as an =Objective<f64>= by the same route
as one defined in this crate, and neither the driver nor =search= can
tell the two apart (cluster_search.rs:9-16).

Key types and functions: =search= (src/methods/cluster_search.rs:116);
=RelaxStats= (cluster_search.rs:33, screen/full/check charged splits,
=screen_share=); =CONVERGED_GRADIENT = 1e-3= (cluster_search.rs:102,
the max-component gradient threshold for calling a relaxation
converged).

Feeds/consumes: consumed by =src/potentials.rs=, =src/python.rs=,
=src/catalog_rpc/server.rs=, and the =molecular_cluster=,
=slab_adsorption= and =cluster_bench= examples as the objective-generic
entry to the cluster driver.

* Learned allocation

Mechanisms that decide, from evidence accumulated during the run itself,
which move or temperature to use next, without a fixed schedule.

** Budget-window temperature

What it is: a closed-form temperature law clamping the design point into
a window where descent and escape are both feasible:
=T = clamp(theta * g / d, b / ln(B + e), 2g / d)=. The ceiling is the
sphere-model descent boundary; the floor is a birth-death escape
requirement (a barrier of depth =b= is not crossed within =B= remaining
proposals below it). On a funnelled landscape the window is often empty,
which the law reports as escape-forced rather than hiding.

Key types and functions: =BudgetWindowTemperature= (src/allocate.rs:34).

Constants and defaults: config knob =theta= (must lie strictly below 2),
=budget_window= toggle (config.rs:86-90). =Config::derived= sets
=theta = 0.5=, i.e. the midpoint of the descent window (config.rs:703-711).

Feeds/consumes: replaces the fixed =temperature= field when enabled; the
gap is measured against the incumbent and the barrier estimated from
uphill steps the chain actually failed to take.

** Floored Thompson move allocator

What it is: a discounted Beta-Bernoulli bandit over move arms, with a
decaying uniform floor =min(1, 1/m)= on round =m= so every arm stays
reachable and the divergent-harmonic-mass restart guarantee (Robbins-
Monro style) holds. Evidence is discounted so the allocator tracks a
landscape whose best move changes as the search moves through it.

Key types and functions: =FlooredThompson= (src/allocate.rs:108);
=beta_draw= (src/allocate.rs:195), a Beta draw built from two Gamma
draws via Marsaglia-Tsang (src/allocate.rs:205), since the crate carries
no dedicated Beta sampler.

Constants and defaults: config knob =allocate_moves=
(config.rs:92). Portfolio-side scheduler constants derived from the
problem rather than tuned: =SLICE_GRAD_EQUIVALENTS = 4=,
=ROUNDS_PER_ARM = 8= (src/methods/portfolio.rs:61-64).

Feeds/consumes: selects among the =MoveLibrary= kernels each hop when
=allocate_moves= is set; reused conceptually by the portfolio driver's
own arm-selection bandit.

** Depth-reward allocator

What it is: rewards move arms by *how deep* the resulting quench went
relative to the run's best known, rather than by acceptance or
improvement alone. Beating the run's best is rare (about five events in
ten thousand hops at 98 points), so an acceptance-rewarded allocator
cannot tell a move that produces deep structures from one that is merely
plausible; measured, this is exactly how an unsuited "twin" arm survives
under acceptance-only reward. Each arm carries a Normal-Gamma posterior
over depth with unknown mean and precision, drawn from via the Student-t
posterior predictive.

Key types and functions: =DepthAllocator= (src/allocate.rs:388).

Constants and defaults: config knob =depth_reward= (config.rs:307-310);
weakly informative prior =kappa = 1e-6=, =alpha = beta = 1.0=
(src/allocate.rs:401-407). On by default in =Config::recommended=
(config.rs:665).

Feeds/consumes: alternative reward signal for the same arm-selection
slot =FlooredThompson= occupies; selected instead of it when
=depth_reward= is set.

** Contextual move allocator

What it is: chooses the move from *where the chain is currently
standing* rather than learning one success rate per move averaged over
every situation. Built for moves with a precondition rather than a rate
(the canonical case: the angular move only applies when a point crosses
a pair-energy criterion, and a rate learned across "applicable" and
"not applicable" situations describes neither).

Key types and functions: =BayesLinear= (src/contextual.rs:47), a
conjugate Bayesian linear model sampled from (distinct from
=crate::screen='s posterior, which is only queried for a tail
probability); =ContextualAllocator= (src/contextual.rs:107).

Constants and defaults: config knobs =contextual_moves=,
=contextual_floor= (config.rs:264-266).

Feeds/consumes: a third alternative in the same arm-selection slot,
context-conditioned rather than global or depth-conditioned.

** Regime selection and arm priors

What it is: classifies a problem's measurable features (box width,
geometry) into a named optimization regime and biases the portfolio's
arm priors and exploitation probability toward the arms known to suit

** Nonequilibrium-umbrella bridge between two minima (=neus_bridge=)

What it is: a string of images through descriptor space between two
already-validated minima, tiling the channel into Voronoi-cell regions
each with its own confined-walker segment budget. Follows
nonequilibrium umbrella sampling (Dickson and Dinner) in its string
form and forward flux sampling in its staging, with the descriptor as
order parameter, so the machinery is engine-agnostic: any segment
runner that propagates and reports descriptors can drive it. The
module is pure state and arithmetic -- no wire, no engine, no
randomness beyond a caller-supplied draw for entry selection
(neus_bridge.rs:14-15). The wiring lives in the cooperative layer:
the coordinator commissions a bridge across the referee's seam at the
population epoch boundary (=commission_bridge=,
src/catalog_rpc/server.rs:1616, storing a =BridgeServerState= of
string, ledger, and entry lists, server.rs:1589) and serves it through
two protocol operations, an assignment poll
(=CatalogOperation::BridgeAssignment=, server.rs:638) and a crossing
report (=CatalogOperation::BridgeCrossing=, server.rs:685). The run
loop consumes it behind the =CATALOG_BRIDGE= gate
(examples/lj_cluster_search.rs:2059): entry states re-enter through
the ordinary probe-proposal path and every region change of the live
descriptor reports as a crossing (=bridge_region_of=,
lj_cluster_search.rs:1797). See
[[file:composition.org][composition.org]] for the role it plays
between chains holding different minima.

Governing equations: weight transfer on an attempted exit from region
\(k\) to a neighbour: \(w_{to} \mathrel{+}= s\, w_{from}\),
\(w_{from} \mathrel{-}= s\, w_{from}\) for transfer fraction
\(s \in (0,1)\), conserving \(\sum_k w_k\) exactly and converging to
flux balance (=WeightLedger::crossing=, neus_bridge.rs:255). The
committor surrogate along the string is the running product of
forward crossing fractions, \(q_k = \prod_{j<k} p_j\) with \(p_j\) the
observed fraction of launches from region \(j\) that reached \(j+1\)
(=committor_surrogate=, neus_bridge.rs:284).

Key types and functions: =BridgeString= (src/methods/neus_bridge.rs:49,
image chain with fixed endpoints); =chord= (neus_bridge.rs:56, initial
equal-spacing construction); =assign=/=chord_distance=
(neus_bridge.rs:86/104, Voronoi region membership and tube-distance
test); =update= (neus_bridge.rs:121, drift toward walker means, three-
point smoothing, equal-arc-length reparametrization, endpoints
pinned); =WeightLedger= (neus_bridge.rs:215, region weights + crossing
counts); =flux_balance_residual= (neus_bridge.rs:297); =EntryLists=
(neus_bridge.rs:321, capped per-region entry-configuration cache for
resuming confined walkers).

Constants and defaults: a bridge string needs at least three images
(=BridgeError::TooFewImages=, neus_bridge.rs:24); the transfer fraction
must lie strictly in (0, 1) (=InvalidTransfer=, neus_bridge.rs:36).

Feeds/consumes: none yet (see above); the natural caller is the

between two catalog basins is the same "known pair of minima" a
boundary crossing already proposes between, and the referee section
below identifies which pairs are seam candidates.

** Spectral referee over the explored landscape (=landscape_graph=)

What it is: the coordinator-side companion to
[[file:catalog.org::*Transition graph and attraction regions][the transition graph]]:
an undirected, weighted graph over census basins built from every
observed =RecordVisit= transition, answering the questions a
coordinator faces at a population-epoch boundary -- is the ensemble
confined, where is the seam, which pair of basins deserves a bridge,
is local work still worth its price (landscape_graph.rs:8-11). Same
Fiedler-vector mathematics as =crate::spectral=
(=TransitionGraph=/=DiffusionDirection=), but a different use:
=spectral= drives a chain-side bias collective variable from one
replica's own visited landscape, while =landscape_graph= is the
coordinator's server-side referee over the whole ensemble's shared
census.

Governing equations: the algebraic connectivity is the second-smallest
eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian \(L = D - W\), recovered by power
iteration on the reflected operator \(B = cI - L\) restricted to the
complement of the constant vector, with \(c\) a Gershgorin bound
\(c = 2\max_i \deg(i) + 1\); the dominant surviving direction is the
Fiedler vector and the eigenvalue is \(c\) minus its Rayleigh quotient
(=spectral_split=, src/methods/landscape_graph.rs:115). Conductance of
the split is cut weight over the smaller side's volume,
\(\phi = \mathrm{cut}(S,\bar S) / \min(\mathrm{vol}(S), \mathrm{vol}(\bar S))\).
Expected passage time to an absorbing target set solves
\((I - Q)\,t = \mathbf{1}\) over the transient (non-absorbing,
target-reachable) basins by dense Gaussian elimination with partial
pivoting, where \(Q\) is the jump chain restricted to them
(=passage_times=, landscape_graph.rs:230-315); unreachable basins
price at infinity by construction, since reachability is separated by
a frontier search before the linear solve.

Key types and functions: =LandscapeGraph= (landscape_graph.rs:52);
=observe_basin=/=observe_crossing= (landscape_graph.rs:75/92,
undirected accumulation: confinement is a property of the seam, not
the direction it was crossed in); =SpectralSplit=
(landscape_graph.rs:34, =algebraic_connectivity=, =left=/=right=
basin sets, =conductance=, =representatives= -- the highest-degree
anchor on each side).

Feeds/consumes: fed by =CatalogOperation::RecordVisit= in
=catalog_rpc::server=, which calls =observe_basin= on every validated
visit and =observe_crossing= when a replica's last basin differs from
its current one (src/catalog_rpc/server.rs:1070-1082, "the census-
visit stream is the referee's evidence"). Consumed by
=referee_community_coverage= (server.rs:1507, holds one family slot
open for a submission on the population plan's currently abandoned
community when conductance stays under 0.1, so resampling cannot
strand a weakly-coupled community once discovered), by
=occupancy_floor= (server.rs, Fiedler split after DECAF labels the
sides, or the landfold community count when the book already holds
a second packing extras have Left: leftover wells of one packing
are one family, a packing seam is two), and by the =observerStatus= reply (server.rs:1964-1997),
which reports =landscape_basins= and a =LandscapeSeam= (algebraic
connectivity, conductance, community sizes, representative basins)
as the seam fields of =CoordinatorStatus= (=landscapeBasins @9=
through =seamRightBasin @15=, schema/Catalog.capnp); rendered as a
=seam= JSON object by =examples/catalog_status.rs:76-104=.

** CSA bank population (=Bank=)

What it is: the diversity-held population under the conformational-
space-annealing replacement rule (Lee, Lee and Scheraga,
arXiv:cond-mat/0307690): a candidate is compared to the bank member it
most resembles, not the worst member, so a near-copy can only displace
its own kind and a bank cannot collapse onto one funnel. A frozen
"first bank", written only during seeding, survives every subsequent
replacement so there is always a distinct population to mix against
even after the working bank converges onto one basin (bank.rs:63-75).

Governing rule: for a candidate at distance \(d\) from its nearest bank
member under threshold \(D_{cut}\): if \(d \leq D_{cut}\), keep the
lower-energy of the two (=Improved=/=Duplicate=); otherwise the
candidate is novel and either fills a free slot (=Added=), displaces
the current worst member if it is better (=Displaced=), or is rejected
(=Rejected=). \(D_{cut}\) is supplied externally by
[[file:catalog.org::*Calibrated merge radius and diversity annealing][the diversity annealer]]
and narrows over the run.

Key types and functions: =Bank= (src/methods/bank.rs:63); =Admission=
(bank.rs:38, the five outcomes above); =offer= (bank.rs:148, the
replacement rule); =seed=/=inject= (bank.rs:206/317, unconditional
adds that also write the frozen first bank); =next_start=
(bank.rs:249, least-used-first selection so starts spread over the

(bank.rs:303, deadlock-injection capacity raise).

** Committor population resampling (=committor_pop=, measured negative)

What it is: *measured negative* -- kept as a reproducibility record
of a mechanism that was built, measured against paired controls, and
refuted (numbers and failure mode in =docs/derivations/=); not part
of the recommended configuration. Each walker in a population carries
a Beta posterior over "a segment from here produces a new lower
minimum before it ends" -- an improvement committor, defined by energy
and history alone, rather than a rare-event committor with a named
product. Resampling is Thompson: the walker with the worst posterior
draw is replaced by a clone of the walker with the best. The point of
contrast is diffusion Monte Carlo depth weighting
(\(\exp(-\beta E)\)), which clones the deepest walker even when that
walker is pinned at the deepest wrong minimum; an improvement-
committor posterior culls a deep-but-dead walker that depth weighting
would multiply instead (committor_pop.rs:9-22).

Key types and functions: =CommittorOutcome= (src/methods/committor_pop.rs:37);
=committor_population= (committor_pop.rs:50, runs =walkers= short

Ledger, run_with_gradient}=; wired into =examples/lj_cluster_search.rs=
as a comparison arm, not into the recommended =Config::recommended=

** Archive-ratcheted exploration (=ffs=, measured negative)

What it is: *measured negative* -- kept as a reproducibility record
of a refuted mechanism (numbers and failure mode in
=docs/derivations/=), not part of the recommended configuration.
Adapts forward flux sampling (Allen, Warren and ten Wolde,
doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.94.018104) to a landscape with many minima
rather than one target: every distinct quenched arrangement, keyed by
its canonical contact graph, is a permanent launch site rather than an
energy-indexed interface cleared on descent, so progress composes
across launches from anywhere (ffs.rs:9-20). Launch sites are picked
by Thompson sampling on a per-site Beta(a, b) discovery posterior ("a
launch from here finds a new key"), tilted toward low energy by the
same temperature the move kernels use.

Governing score: for each launchable site (energy within
=window * temperature= of the best), score
\(= \ln(\text{Beta draw}) - (E_{site} - E_{best}) / T\); the site
with the highest score is launched from (ffs.rs:127-146).

Key types and functions: =FfsConfig= (src/methods/ffs.rs:51,
=window=6=, =shots=24=, =key_cutoff=1.35=); =FfsOutcome= (ffs.rs:36);
=ffs_descent= (ffs.rs:96, the Thompson-launch loop over a
=HashMap<u64, Site>= archive keyed by =crate::graphkey::contact_key=).

Feeds/consumes: consumes =crate::graphkey::contact_key= and
=crate::methods::cluster_hopping::{ClusterMove, Ledger,

comparison arm.

** Nested search under a descending ceiling (=nested=, measured negative)

What it is: *measured negative* -- kept as a reproducibility record
of a refuted mechanism (numbers and failure mode in
=docs/derivations/=), not part of the recommended configuration.
Applies nested sampling (Skilling, doi:10.1214/06-BA127; Partay,
Bartok and Csanyi, doi:10.1021/jp1012973 for quenched landscapes) to
the cluster driver: hold =K= live points, repeatedly discard the
worst and replace it with a clone of a survivor walked under the hard
constraint \(E < E_{worst}\). No Metropolis ratio appears; every state
below the ceiling is equally admissible, which keeps precursor
structures above the incumbent best alive across a funnel-crossing
excursion that a product-of-acceptance-probabilities chain would
reject piecewise (nested.rs:24-38).

Governing relations: each replacement compresses the reachable
log-volume by \(-1/K\) in expectation (an Exponential(K) decrement),
so \(\ln V\) after death \(i\) has closed-form mean and variance from
order statistics; the local slope \(d\ln V/dE\), fit by least squares
over the last window of deaths, is the reciprocal statistical
temperature of the minima distribution at the current ceiling, and
feeds a stopping rule that starts a fresh population once the
posterior mass still reachable below the incumbent best falls under a
threshold (nested.rs:300-314, =VolumeCurve=).

Key types and functions: =NestedConfig= (src/methods/nested.rs:69,
=live=32=, =walk=8=); =NestedOutcome= (nested.rs:47);
=nested_search= (nested.rs:107); =VolumeCurve= (nested.rs:316,
=record=/=log_volume=/=slope=/=mass_below=, the volume-energy
posterior).

Feeds/consumes: consumes =crate::methods::cluster_hopping::{ClusterMove,
Ledger, random_cluster}=; wired into =examples/lj_cluster_search.rs=
as a comparison arm.

** Rank-1 additive-independence sampler (=additive_independence_sa=)

What it is: the unified MCMC+SA point enabled by a separable
surrogate. A pilot fits an =AdditiveSurrogate= over all =d=
coordinates from a uniform design; each temperature epoch then draws
a block of proposals two ways -- a global independence move sampling
every coordinate's own tempered marginal by independent per-coordinate
inverse-CDF draws from the surrogate, and a local Gaussian random walk
around the incumbent -- and every proposal is Metropolis-accepted
against the *true* objective, which removes the mean-field bias the
surrogate introduces. Because the surrogate factorises across
coordinates for a separable objective, one global draw places every
coordinate at its own tempered optimum at once, a regime a local
random-walk proposal (efficiency decaying like \(1/d\)) cannot reach
(additive_independence.rs:1-14).

Governing acceptance: geometric temperature schedule
\(T(\text{frac}) = T_{hi} (T_{lo}/T_{hi})^{\text{frac}}\),
\(T_{hi} = \max(|f_x|, 1)\), \(T_{lo} = 10^{-3} T_{hi}\); Metropolis
accept when \(f_y \leq f_x\) or with probability
\(\exp(-(f_y-f_x)/T)\) (additive_independence.rs:100-139).

Key types and functions: =AdditiveIndependenceResult=
(src/methods/additive_independence.rs:24); =additive_independence_sa=
(additive_independence.rs:41, pilot capped at half the budget, degree
and inverse-CDF grid resolution as caller parameters).

Feeds/consumes: consumes =eindir_core::AdditiveSurrogate=; exposed
directly through =src/python.rs= as a standalone driver, not folded
into =portfolio=.

** Whitened BFWT annealed descent, standalone (=amsa=)

What it is: the portfolio's =am_sa= arm exposed as a standalone
solver so it can be measured on its own outside the bandit. Pairs the
budget-feasible window temperature (below) with Haario covariance
whitening: on a general quadratic the descent window collapses by a
factor of order \(d/\kappa\) along soft eigendirections, and a
whitened proposal restores \(\theta_c = 2\) (amsa.rs:1-9). Runs three
quarters of the budget as a single adaptive chain with Robbins-Monro
proposal-scale control, an online barrier estimate from rejected
uphill moves, and IPOP-style reseeding on stagnation; the final
quarter runs the stall-recovering projected quasi-Newton polish when a
gradient is available.

Governing law: proposal covariance is the running (Welford) scatter
regularized as
\(\hat\Sigma + \epsilon\,\mathrm{diag}(\text{width}^2)\), Cholesky-
factored (=proposal_chol=, src/methods/amsa.rs:97, falling back to a
box-scaled identity before \(2d\) accepted observations); the
Robbins-Monro scale update is
\(\log(\text{scale}) \mathrel{+}= (a - \alpha^*)/\sqrt{n}\) toward
\(\alpha^* = 0.32\) (=AM_ALPHA_TARGET=, amsa.rs:41); a 15% draw
probability substitutes a per-coordinate Cauchy jump for the whitened
Gaussian step, since well-to-well escape on separable multi-well
(Schwefel-class) landscapes needs coordinate flips no single radial
jump in the whitened metric supplies (=TAIL_JUMP_PROB=, amsa.rs:175).

Key types and functions: =AmsaResult= (amsa.rs:157); =amsa_optimize=
(amsa.rs:190); =AmSaState= (amsa.rs:21, mean/scatter/log_scale/
barrier_hat/reseed_gen); =cholesky_lower= (amsa.rs:133).

Feeds/consumes: consumes
[[file:catalog.org::*Budget-feasible window temperature (=bfwt=, D11)][=bfwt::budget_feasible_temp=]]
and =local_polish::projected_gradient_polish=; consumed by
=portfolio= as the =am_sa= arm and exposed directly through
=src/python.rs=.

** Automatic Bayesian chain mixing (=bayesian_mixing=)

What it is: a chain-count-free wrapper around any =Sampler<T>= with
one visible knob, a total proposal budget. Chain count, incumbent
protection, and per-proposal chain selection are all derived online
from a Beta-Bernoulli posterior over "the next proposal on this chain
improves the global best". Chains start from a quasi-Monte-Carlo
design when the sampler exposes QMC bounds.

Governing rule: chain count
\(n = \mathrm{clamp}(\min(\lfloor B/64 \rfloor, \lceil\sqrt{d}\rceil), 2, 4)\)
(=auto_chain_count=, src/methods/bayesian_mixing.rs:160); chain
selection draws a Beta(\(\alpha_c\), \(\beta_c\)) sample per chain and
switches away from the incumbent chain only when the challenger's
draw beats the incumbent's own fresh draw by more than 0.05
(=select_chain=, bayesian_mixing.rs:166, a resampled-incumbent
comparison that damps switching noise).

Key types and functions: =BayesianMixingSampler<S: Sampler<f64>>=
(bayesian_mixing.rs:38); =BayesianMixingResult= (bayesian_mixing.rs:17,

(bayesian_mixing.rs:56).

Feeds/consumes: generic over any =Sampler<f64>= impl; re-exported at

=tests/bayesian_mixing.rs=; no example or portfolio-arm call site
found in this scan (contrast =mcmc_sa= below, which is referenced from
=src/hmc/nuts.rs= even without a call site).

** Bayesian pilot for SA/GSA hyperparameters (=bayesian_pilot=)

What it is: replaces hand-picked or grid-searched \((T_0, \sigma,
q_v)\) with a Laplace-approximated Bayesian pilot: run =n_pilot=
chains at prior draws, observe acceptance and improvement, fit a 3x3
Hessian-inverse Laplace posterior over \((\log T_0, \log\sigma, q_v)\),
and use the MAP estimate for the production run. \(q_v\) is the
GSA visiting-index model-selection axis: \(q_v=1\) is BSA
(Boltzmann/Gaussian), \(q_v=2\) is FSA (Cauchy), and \((1,3)\)
interpolates continuously; the pilot fits the exponent to the
objective's geometry instead of committing to a fixed literature
point (bayesian_pilot.rs:1-13). When the production phase freezes the
MAP hyperparameters, Hajek 1988 (doi:10.1287/moor.13.2.311) gives a.s.
convergence to the global optimum unchanged; unfrozen online
adaptation instead needs the diminishing-adaptation condition of
Roberts and Rosenthal 2007 (doi:10.1239/jap/1183667414).

Key types and functions: =PilotObservation=
(src/methods/bayesian_pilot.rs:47); =LaplacePosterior=
(bayesian_pilot.rs:64); =PilotPrior= (bayesian_pilot.rs:85, log-Normal
on \(T_0\)/\(\sigma\), truncated scaled-Beta on \(q_v\) over
=(Q_V_MIN, Q_V_MAX) = (1.05, 2.95)=); =fit_laplace=, =pilot_draws=,
=pilot_draws_qmc= (re-exported src/methods/mod.rs:65-68).

Constants and defaults: =TARGET_ACCEPT_RATE = 0.234=
(bayesian_pilot.rs:38, the Roberts/Rosenthal 2001 asymptotically
optimal random-walk-Metropolis rate SA at high \(T\) is matched
against).

Feeds/consumes: consumed by =portfolio= as a hyperparameter-fitting
stage ahead of the SA arms.

** Budget-feasible window temperature (=bfwt=, D11)

What it is: a temperature law combining a D6 descent ceiling
\(T_{hi} = 2\,\mathrm{gap}/d\) with a D7 escape floor
\(T_{lo} = \hat b / \ln(B_{rem} + e)\) and a design interior
\(T_{des} = \theta^* \mathrm{gap}/d\), \(\theta^*=1/2\): the
temperature clamps \(T_{des}\) into the window \([T_{lo}, T_{hi}]\)
when the window is nonempty (\(T_{lo} < T_{hi}\)), and forces
\(T = T_{lo}\) otherwise. As the barrier estimate \(\hat b \to 0\)
this recovers GPMD's \(T = T_{des}\) exactly (bfwt.rs:1-11). Explicitly
a local law under stated models, not a global solver.

Governing equations:
\(T_{hi} = 2\max(\mathrm{gap}, \epsilon)/d\) (=t_hi=, bfwt.rs:58);
\(T_{des} = \theta^* \max(\mathrm{gap},\epsilon)/d\) (=t_des=,
bfwt.rs:65); \(T_{lo} = \max(\hat b, 0)/\ln(\max(B_{rem},0)+e)\)
(=t_lo=, bfwt.rs:72); window nonempty iff
\(\hat b\, d < 2\,\mathrm{gap}\,\ln(B_{rem}+e)\) (=window_nonempty=,
bfwt.rs:79); mode selection and clamp (=budget_feasible_temp=,
bfwt.rs:87-109, returns one of =Design=/=EscapeFloor=/=DescentCap=/
=EscapeForced=).

Key types and functions: =BfwtMode= (bfwt.rs:33); =BfwtResult=
(bfwt.rs:113); =budget_feasible_temp= (bfwt.rs:87);
=THETA_STAR = 0.5=, =EULER_E= (bfwt.rs:23-25).

Feeds/consumes: consumed by =amsa= (above) as the temperature law
inside its adaptive chain, and by =portfolio= as its own arm.

** GLE-thermostatted Langevin annealing (=gle_langevin=)

What it is: the colored-noise point of the typed algebra: a
gradient-driven Move slot running BAB Langevin dynamics under a
generalized Langevin equation thermostat (Ceriotti-Bussi-Parrinello)
rather than single white-noise friction. A white-noise thermostat
critically damps one frequency, so an ill-conditioned objective (a
wide spread of Hessian curvatures) decorrelates slowly on most modes;
the GLE shapes a frequency-dependent friction from a few auxiliary
momenta to flatten sampling efficiency across the curvature band,
while temperature annealing settles the trajectory into a basin.
Conditioning is handled by the noise spectrum exactly as a
\(1/\sqrt{D}\) proposal scale handles dimension -- both are Move-slot
transforms any driver could read (gle_langevin.rs:1-14).

** MCMC-style multi-chain SA with Gelman-Rubin termination (=mcmc_sa=)

What it is: the MCMC-tradition point of the typed algebra, contrasted
with classical SA's fixed inner-loop count \(K\): at fixed temperature
both are the identical Metropolis-Hastings kernel with Boltzmann-Gibbs
stationary distribution, differing only in loop control. This module
runs until a between-chain Gelman-Rubin diagnostic crosses convergence,
then advances the temperature epoch, using the same fixed-temperature
kernel as
[[file:catalog.org::*Parallel tempering / replica exchange][parallel tempering]]
with a different epoch-stopping rule (mcmc_sa.rs:1-14).

Governing statistic: per-coordinate Gelman-Rubin
\(\hat R = \sqrt{\hat V/W}\) with within-chain variance \(W\),
between-chain variance \(B\), and pooled estimate
\(\hat V = \frac{N-1}{N} W + \frac{B}{N}\) over \(M\) chains of length
\(N\); the reported \(\hat R\) is the maximum over coordinates, since
SA practice converges on the worst-mixing coordinate
(=GelmanRubin::compute=, src/methods/mcmc_sa.rs:45).

Key types and functions: =MultiChainState= (mcmc_sa.rs:24);
=GelmanRubin= (mcmc_sa.rs:40); =MultiChainSampler=/=MultiChainResult=
(re-exported src/methods/mod.rs:91).

Feeds/consumes: generic over any =Sampler<f64>= impl; re-exported at
the crate root (src/lib.rs:178-179), referenced from
src/hmc/nuts.rs:288 ("=NutsSaSampler= drops into =run_rs= and
=MultiChainSampler= like =HmcSaSampler='"), and exercised by
=tests/mcmc_sa.rs= and =tests/nuts_sa.rs=; no portfolio-arm or example

** Multimodality routing probe (=routing_probe=)

What it is: evidence-gathering for portfolio regime routing. A static
mean-box-width rule cannot separate a genuinely multi-basin box
(Styblinski-class) from a single deep least-squares valley of the same
width, and the two want opposite regimes (global heavy-tailed
visiting versus deep local descent). This module spends a small,
ledger-charged probe budget on short projected-gradient descents from
spread starts and counts distinct basin-depth classes among the
endpoints; routing commits to the global regime only when at least two
competitive basins are found (routing_probe.rs:1-10).

Governing rule: two descent endpoints belong to the same depth class
when their gap is at most =BASIN_DEPTH_REL_TOL = 1e-2= of the probe's
own depth scale
\(\max(|f_{worst\_start} - f_{best\_descent}|, |f_{best\_descent}|, 1)\)
(=distinct_depth_classes=, src/methods/routing_probe.rs:50, scale-
and-offset invariant by construction).

Key types and functions: =RoutingProbe= (routing_probe.rs:18,
=multimodal= is =distinct_basins >= 2=); =multimodality_probe=
(routing_probe.rs:74).

Feeds/consumes: consumes =local_polish::projected_gradient_polish=;
consumed by =portfolio= for regime routing (compare
[[file:catalog.org::*Regime selection and arm priors][regime selection]]).

** Sketch-map embedding (=sketchmap=)

What it is: a landmark fit to a 2-D embedding plus cheap out-of-sample
projection, adapted from the sketch-map dimensionality reduction of
Ceriotti, Tribello and Parrinello (JCTC 2011, cosmo-epfl/sketchmap).
High-D and low-D pairwise distances pass through sigmoid switch
functions so only intermediate length scales contribute to the
embedding stress, preserving local neighborhoods while discarding
uninformative long-range metric noise. Built for MetaD collective
variables and TPS basin geometry, not as a full MD trajectory-analysis
stack (sketchmap.rs:1-10).

Governing equations: high-D switch
\(F(r) = 1 - \left(1 + (2^{a/b}-1)(r/\sigma)^a\right)^{-b/a}\)
(=sigmoid_switch=, src/methods/sketchmap.rs:31), with the low-D switch
\(G\) the same form under its own \((a',b',\sigma')\); embedding
stress \(\sum_{i<j} w_{ij} (F(D_{ij}) - G(d_{ij}))^2\) over landmark
pairs; an out-of-sample point is placed by minimizing stress against
the landmarks with high-D distances held fixed.

Key types and functions: =SketchMap2d= (sketchmap.rs:88);
=pairwise_l2=, =row_l2=, =median_pairwise=, =farthest_point_landmarks=
(re-exported src/methods/mod.rs:101-104).

Constants and defaults: =DEFAULT_A = 4.0=, =DEFAULT_B = 2.0=,
=DEFAULT_A_LOW = DEFAULT_B_LOW = 2.0= (sketchmap.rs:21-27, sketch-map
literature defaults).

Feeds/consumes: consumed by =portfolio= for collective-variable
projection; no direct call site found outside =portfolio= and its own
tests in this scan.

** Transition-path shooting moves (=tps_shoot=)

What it is: continuous-box adaptation of transition-path shooting from
pnastps (TYCeTS / PNAS 2019, =pnastps-master/code/tps-only_pnas/main.cpp=).
The source algorithm maintains a path of frames between reactant and
product basins distinguished by an order-parameter threshold, shoots
by re-propagating a random interior frame forward or backward and
accepting if the trial path stays reactive, and shifts by extending
one end while dropping the other. Here path frames are box points, the
path is a linear (or noised) interpolation between two archived
basin endpoints, the order parameter defaults to the true objective
value along the path (or a geometric near-seed test), and shooting
re-propagates half-paths by isotropic noise plus optional local
descent (tps_shoot.rs:1-27).

Key types and functions: =linear_path= (src/methods/tps_shoot.rs:35);
=path_is_reactive= (tps_shoot.rs:59, classical OP-threshold

OP variant, lower is better); =path_reactive_geometric=
(tps_shoot.rs:82, endpoint-proximity variant); =ShootDirection=,
=apply_shoot=, =accept_reactive_shoot=, =pick_shoot_direction=,
=pick_shoot_index=, =best_frame_index= (re-exported
src/methods/mod.rs:106-109).

Feeds/consumes: consumed by =portfolio= as a comparison arm; every
evaluated frame charges the true objective so the best point on
accepted or trial paths is available for incumbent updates.
